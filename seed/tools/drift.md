## What is Drift?

Drift is the chatbot that popularised conversational marketing, and it is now part of Salesloft, which acquired the company in 2024 and folded it into its revenue platform. That ownership shapes the product. Drift is not a support tool that happens to have a bot; it is a sales tool that lives in a chat widget. The bot greets visitors, asks qualifying questions, checks whether the visitor belongs to a target account, and either books a meeting on a rep's calendar or routes the conversation to a live salesperson.

The platform includes playbooks, which are branching conversation flows tied to page, campaign or account criteria, a visitor identification layer that de-anonymises companies from IP and enrichment data, and a live chat inbox for reps. AI features draft bot responses from your site content and score conversations by intent. Pricing is annual, quote based and priced for companies with a real sales team, and there is no free plan.

## Where it shines

Drift is excellent at its actual job, which is turning high-intent website traffic into booked meetings. The calendar booking flow inside the chat window is smooth, the routing rules can send a conversation to the account owner in the CRM rather than whoever is online, and the whole thing is designed around pipeline rather than resolution. Marketing teams see the meetings and pipeline attributed in the CRM, which makes the cost easier to defend than most chat tools.

The account-based features are where the Salesloft ownership helps. Drift identifies known companies on the site, fires alerts to reps in Slack or the Salesloft inbox when a target account is browsing pricing, and lets you show a different playbook to a named account than to an anonymous visitor. Integrations with Salesforce, HubSpot, Marketo, 6sense and Demandbase are mature because this is the audience Drift was built for.

For a sales rep, the live chat experience is good. They see the visitor's company, pages viewed and past conversations, and can hand off to a meeting or a sequence in Salesloft without leaving the thread.

## Where it falls short

Drift is the wrong tool for customer support. There is no ticketing, no knowledge base worth the name, no SLAs and no order or account data in the agent view. Teams that bought it for marketing and then tried to run support through it usually end up adding [Intercom](/tools/intercom/) or [Freshchat](/tools/freshchat/) and keeping Drift for the marketing site only.

Price is the second problem. Drift has always been one of the most expensive chat products on the market, contracts are annual and the entry point assumes a sales organisation with several reps. Small companies get better value from [Crisp](/tools/crisp/) or the HubSpot chat tools they may already own.

The Salesloft acquisition has also created uncertainty. Development has shifted towards integration with the Salesloft platform, standalone Drift customers report slower feature progress, and the AI chatbot has not kept pace with dedicated agents such as [Ada](/tools/ada/) or Fin. Buyers should ask directly about the roadmap for standalone use before signing.

## Conclusion

Drift remains the strongest conversational marketing bot for B2B companies that want the website to book meetings with target accounts, and it makes the most sense for teams already using or considering Salesloft. It does not belong on a support shortlist. If your goal is resolving customer questions rather than generating pipeline, buy something else and keep Drift, if at all, on the marketing site.
