## What is Otter?

Otter is a transcription service that started as a phone app for recording lectures and interviews and has grown into a meeting assistant. OtterPilot, its bot, joins Zoom, Google Meet and Microsoft Teams calls from your calendar, records them, and produces a live transcript that you can watch scroll during the meeting. When the call ends it writes a summary, pulls out action items and assigns them to the people named in the conversation.

The transcript is the heart of the product. Speakers are identified and labelled, you can highlight passages while the meeting is still running, and the audio is synced to the text so clicking a sentence plays that moment. Otter AI Chat lets you ask questions about a single meeting or across all your recordings, such as what a client said about budget last month. Free accounts get a monthly allowance of transcription minutes and a cap on the length of each recording. Paid tiers raise those limits and add team features such as shared workspaces and admin controls.

## Where it shines

Transcription accuracy in English is very good for clear audio, and Otter is one of the few tools that shows the transcript in real time rather than after processing. That matters for anyone taking notes during a call and for participants who are hard of hearing. The mobile app is also stronger than most competitors, so in-person meetings, site visits and interviews recorded on a phone get the same treatment as video calls.

The pricing model is the other reason to pick Otter. The free tier is enough for a light user to try it properly, and the entry paid plan is cheap relative to the field. For a freelancer, a recruiter running a dozen screens a week, or a small team that just wants transcripts and a summary in the inbox, the cost per meeting is hard to beat.

Action item extraction is reasonably reliable and the summaries are short and readable rather than padded. Otter can post the summary to a Slack channel or a shared folder, and the highlight and comment tools make it easy to point a colleague at the two minutes of a call that actually matter.

## Where it falls short

Otter is weaker the moment you want the notes to go somewhere structured. Integrations with CRMs and project management tools are thin compared with [Fireflies](/tools/fireflies/), which is built around pushing meeting data into Salesforce, HubSpot and task trackers. If the goal is a sales team whose calls automatically update deal records, Otter is not the right tool.

Language support is limited. English is the strong suit, a small number of other languages are supported, and mixed language meetings are handled poorly. Accuracy also drops noticeably with crosstalk, background noise or heavily accented speakers. That is true of every tool in this category, but it is less forgiven when the product is positioned on transcript quality.

The bot behaviour has irritated plenty of users. By default OtterPilot can join every meeting on the calendar, including ones you did not intend to record, and it can email meeting participants afterwards unless settings are changed. Both are adjustable, but the defaults lean towards growth rather than discretion. Enterprise controls for retention and data residency also lag behind what larger organisations expect.

## Conclusion

Otter is the sensible pick for individuals and small teams who want dependable transcripts, decent summaries and a low bill. The real-time transcript and the strong mobile app set it apart from the rest of the field. Teams that need meeting data flowing into a CRM, or that work in several languages, should look at Fireflies instead. Check the bot settings before your first call, or it will introduce itself to everyone on your calendar.
