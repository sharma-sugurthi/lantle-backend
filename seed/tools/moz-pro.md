## What is Moz Pro?

Moz Pro is one of the oldest subscription SEO toolsets still in active development. It bundles Keyword Explorer for query research, a rank tracker organised around Campaigns, a site crawler that reports technical issues by severity, Link Explorer for backlink research, and on-page grading that scores a URL against a target keyword. Moz also maintains Domain Authority and Page Authority, the link based scores that many marketers still quote as shorthand for site strength, and MozBar, a free browser extension that overlays those metrics on search results.

The product sits on a tiered monthly subscription with limits on tracked keywords, crawled pages and campaigns, and a free trial before you commit. Moz sells a separate local listings product for businesses managing Google Business Profiles, and offers an API for pulling link metrics into your own systems. A community Q and A forum, a large library of beginner guides and a certification programme round out what is as much an education brand as a software product.

## Where it shines

Approachability is the main draw. Compared with the denser research suites, Moz Pro explains what each metric means in plain language, prioritises issues rather than dumping every finding on you, and structures the interface around the questions a small team actually asks: what should we target, how are we ranking, and what is broken on the site. New hires get productive faster here than almost anywhere else.

Keyword Explorer has a useful Priority score that blends volume, difficulty, organic click through and your own relevance rating, which helps when you need to rank a long list of candidates rather than analyse a single term. The keyword suggestions are decent for English markets and the SERP analysis shows who ranks alongside their authority scores.

The site crawler is solid for typical business sites. It runs on a schedule, highlights new issues since the last crawl, and groups findings so a developer can work through them without a lecture on SEO. On-page grading gives writers a checklist they can act on without needing an SEO specialist in the room.

Domain Authority remains widely understood. When you need to report link strength to a client or executive who has heard the term, Moz is where the number comes from, and the free tools let you check it without a subscription.

## Where it falls short

The backlink index is smaller and refreshes more slowly than the market leaders. For serious link building or competitive link analysis, [Ahrefs](/tools/ahrefs/) shows more referring domains, catches new links sooner and offers far richer filtering. Moz Pro is fine for a high level view but not for the day to day work of a link team.

Keyword data outside English speaking markets is thinner, and volume figures can lag. Teams working across European or Asian markets often find [Semrush](/tools/semrush/) more reliable for local research and for anything to do with paid search, which Moz barely touches.

The pace of development has been slow for a product of its age. Features such as an SEO content editor, AI visibility tracking and deeper competitive intelligence arrived later and lighter than at competing suites, and rank tracking allowances on the lower tiers are tight for agencies.

## Conclusion

Moz Pro is a comfortable, well explained SEO toolkit for a small team or a marketer learning the discipline, and Domain Authority keeps it relevant in client conversations. Its weaknesses are depth and speed: the link index and international data trail the leaders, and heavy users will outgrow it. Pick it for clarity and gentle onboarding, not for the most complete dataset.
