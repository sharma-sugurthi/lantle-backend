## What is Inventory Planner?

Inventory Planner is a demand forecasting and replenishment tool for ecommerce brands. It connects to Shopify, Amazon, WooCommerce, BigCommerce and a range of ERPs and warehouse systems, pulls in sales history and current stock, then forecasts how much of each SKU you will sell over the coming months and tells you what to reorder, in what quantity, and when. Purchase orders can be raised inside the tool, sent to suppliers and tracked through to receipt. The company is now part of Sage, though the product still works as a standalone subscription.

The core screen is the replenishment report. For every variant it shows forecast demand, days of stock remaining, the recommended order quantity and the date you need to place the order to avoid a stockout, taking supplier lead times into account. Forecasts are generated from historical sales with seasonality, trend and stockout periods considered, and you can override them by SKU or apply growth assumptions across a category when you know something the data does not, such as a planned campaign.

## Where it shines

The replenishment logic is thoughtful in the ways that matter to operators. Lead time, review period and safety stock are set per supplier and per warehouse, and the tool recognises when a product was out of stock and should not treat that gap as zero demand. Bundles and assemblies are handled properly, so components are forecast from the bundles that consume them. Multi-location businesses can plan transfers between warehouses as well as purchases from vendors.

The purchase order workflow saves real time. Recommendations become a draft PO in a couple of clicks, grouped by supplier, with minimum order quantities and case pack sizes applied. Once sent, the PO tracks what is on order so the forecast accounts for incoming stock, and partial receipts update the numbers. For teams that previously built POs in spreadsheets from exported reports, this alone justifies the subscription.

Reporting on overstock and dead stock is as useful as the reorder side. The tool quantifies how much cash is tied up in slow movers, which supports markdown decisions and conversations with finance. Open-to-buy planning for teams working to seasonal budgets is available and links top-down budgets to SKU-level orders.

Integration coverage is wide, and the Shopify connection in particular is mature, syncing variants, locations and cost data reliably.

## Where it falls short

The forecast is statistical, not magical. Products with short or erratic history, new launches and heavily promoted items produce poor forecasts unless someone overrides them, and that means an operator still needs to review recommendations rather than approve them blindly. Fashion brands with short product lifecycles find the historical approach less helpful than brands with a stable core range.

Pricing is based on business volume rather than a flat subscription, so fast-growing stores see their bill climb, and small stores can find it expensive relative to the value at low SKU counts. The interface is dense; there are many settings per product and per supplier, and initial configuration takes days rather than hours if you want accurate results. Support is responsive but the setup burden is mostly on you.

It is also purely a planning tool. It does not manage the warehouse, pick and pack, or route orders. Storefront platforms such as [Shopify](/tools/shopify/) supply the sales data and basic stock counts; Inventory Planner sits above that and decides what to buy. If your bigger issue is that competitors keep undercutting you and stock is moving slowly for that reason, [Prisync](/tools/prisync/) addresses the pricing side.

## Conclusion

Inventory Planner is the most capable replenishment tool built specifically for multichannel ecommerce, and for brands with a stable catalogue and meaningful order volume it turns purchasing from a spreadsheet chore into a managed process. Very small stores and brands with fast-turning, unpredictable ranges will get less from the forecasts and should weigh the cost carefully.
