## What is HubSpot Sales Hub?

HubSpot Sales Hub is the sales side of the HubSpot platform, built on the same shared CRM that the marketing, service and content products use. Contacts, companies and deals live in one database, and every email, call, meeting and form submission attaches to the right record without anyone doing data entry. The free tier includes the CRM itself, basic deal pipelines, email tracking and a meeting scheduler, which is why so many small companies start here before they have decided on a sales process at all.

The paid tiers add the tools that turn it into a working outbound and pipeline system: multi-step email sequences, calling from inside the record, playbooks, quotes, forecasting, custom reporting and workflow automation. Breeze, HubSpot's AI layer, drafts emails, summarises call recordings, scores leads and can research a prospect's company before a rep opens the record. It works from inside Gmail and Outlook through a browser extension, so most reps rarely need to open the HubSpot interface directly during the day.

## Where it shines

Adoption is the real strength. Reps who resisted logging activity in other systems tend to keep HubSpot current because the logging is passive: send an email from Gmail and it is on the contact record, book a meeting through the scheduling link and the deal stage can move automatically. Managers get pipeline reports that reflect reality rather than what was updated the night before a forecast call.

The scheduling and sequence tools are good enough that most small teams do not need separate products for them. Sequences support personalisation tokens, task steps and automatic unenrolment when a prospect replies, and the meeting links handle round robin assignment for teams that share inbound demos.

Reporting improved a lot in recent years. The custom report builder can join contact, company and deal data with activity records, and the forecasting tool lets managers submit adjusted numbers alongside the rep's weighted pipeline. For companies that also run HubSpot marketing, attribution from first touch to closed deal is available without a data warehouse.

The onboarding and documentation are the best in the category. HubSpot Academy, community answers and in-app guidance mean a two person team can configure pipelines and automation without a consultant.

## Where it falls short

Pricing climbs steeply once you need the Professional tier, and the per seat cost plus a mandatory onboarding fee is a jump from the free product. Features that feel essential, such as sequences and custom reporting, sit behind that step, so the free tier is more of a trial than a long term home for a real sales team.

The contact database is only as good as what you put in it. HubSpot does not include a meaningful third party data source, so prospecting still depends on a separate provider. Teams that run outbound at volume usually pair it with [Apollo](/tools/apollo/) for contact data and sequencing, or build enrichment flows in [Clay](/tools/clay/) and push the results into HubSpot.

Customisation has limits compared with Salesforce. Complex multi-object data models, territory rules and heavy approval logic are possible but awkward, and companies with large enterprise sales operations tend to outgrow it. Call recording and coaching exist in the higher tiers, but they are shallow next to a dedicated product like [Gong](/tools/gong/).

## Conclusion

HubSpot Sales Hub is the sensible default CRM for small and mid-sized teams, especially ones that already run HubSpot for marketing. It wins on adoption, ease of setup and the fact that email, meetings, sequences and reporting come from one vendor. Budget for the Professional tier from the start, plan on a separate data provider for outbound, and expect to revisit the decision if you grow into a complex enterprise sales motion.
