## What is ElevenLabs?

ElevenLabs is a voice AI platform. The original product was text to speech that sounded convincingly human, and that remains the core, but the surface area has grown well beyond it. You can clone a voice from a short sample or, with more audio and a verification step, build a professional clone that is hard to tell from the source. A speech to speech mode lets an actor record a line and have it re-voiced in another voice while keeping the timing and emotion. The dubbing studio takes a finished video, transcribes it, translates it and re-voices each speaker in the target language, with a timeline for correcting anything the automatic pass got wrong.

Studio is the long-form environment for audiobooks, podcasts and narrated courses, with chapters, multiple voices and pronunciation dictionaries. A separate agents platform handles two-way voice conversations: you connect a language model and a knowledge base, choose a voice, and deploy to a phone number or a website widget. Sound effect and music generation round out the catalogue. Pricing is a freemium subscription metered in characters or credits per month, with the free tier covering light personal use and requiring attribution. An API and SDKs cover every capability.

## Where it shines

Voice quality is the reason to be here. Narration comes out with natural pacing, breath and emphasis, and the multilingual models switch language mid-sentence without the robotic edge that older engines had. For a content team producing explainer videos or an internal training library, it removes the studio booking entirely.

The voice library is large and well labelled, and the licensing model means you can find a professional voice, use it commercially and know the original speaker is being compensated. Building a consistent brand voice across dozens of assets is straightforward once you have picked one.

Dubbing is faster than any manual pipeline. A ten minute product video can be in five languages within the hour, and because the editor exposes the transcript and timing, a native speaker can fix awkward phrasing without going back to an agency. It is not broadcast grade without review, but for marketing and support content it is more than adequate.

The agents product is one of the more complete options for voice bots. Latency is low enough for a natural back and forth, interruption handling works, and the tool calling and knowledge base features mean you can build something useful without stitching together three vendors.

## Where it falls short

Costs scale with volume. Long-form audio, dubbing and always-on agents burn through character and minute quotas quickly, and the price per unit on the lower tiers is high. Estimate your monthly output before choosing a plan, and expect to move up a tier sooner than you planned.

Mispronunciations still happen, particularly with brand names, acronyms and technical vocabulary. The pronunciation dictionary fixes them, but somebody has to listen to the output and maintain that list. Emotional direction is also indirect: you shape delivery through punctuation, context and voice settings rather than explicit stage directions, and getting a specific read can take several regenerations.

Voice cloning carries governance overhead. You need documented consent, and the professional clone process requires the original speaker to verify. For teams producing video where visuals matter as much as sound, [Runway](/tools/runway/) covers the footage side and pairs naturally with ElevenLabs for narration. Building a voice agent still requires engineering time to connect it to your systems.

## Conclusion

ElevenLabs is the default choice for generated speech and the most capable all-round voice platform for business use. Text to speech, dubbing and long-form production are strong today, and the agents product is maturing fast. Watch the metering closely and budget for review time on pronunciation, but if voice is part of your output, this is where to start.
