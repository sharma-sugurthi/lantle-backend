## What is Workable?

Workable is an applicant tracking system aimed at small and mid-sized companies. You write a job description once, and Workable publishes it to a large number of free and paid job boards in a single step, then pulls every application back into one pipeline. Candidates move through stages you define, hiring managers score them against a shared set of criteria, and interview scheduling syncs with Google or Microsoft calendars so nobody has to trade emails to find a slot.

The platform also includes sourcing tools. AI Recruiter searches Workable's candidate database and the wider web for passive profiles that match a role, and the browser extension lets you pull LinkedIn profiles straight into a pipeline. Careers page hosting, offer letters, e-signatures and a basic referral programme are built in. More recently Workable has added HR features such as employee profiles, time off and onboarding, so it can serve as a lightweight HRIS for companies that have nothing else in place.

## Where it shines

Setup speed is the main reason small teams pick Workable. A hiring manager with no recruiting background can post a role, syndicate it, and start reviewing applicants within an hour. The interface hides most of the configuration that enterprise systems expose by default, which is exactly what a founder or office manager who hires twice a quarter wants.

The job board distribution is broad and it works. Free postings go to the major aggregators automatically, and paid slots on premium boards can be bought from inside the product without setting up separate accounts. For a team without a sourcing function, the volume of inbound applicants this generates is usually enough to fill most non-specialist roles.

Collaboration features are practical rather than elaborate. Scorecards push interviewers to leave structured feedback, comments and mentions keep the discussion attached to the candidate record, and the mobile app is good enough to review and approve on the move. The AI-assisted job description writer and screening question generator save real time for people who do not write postings often.

Pricing is transparent, with monthly plans that can be paused when you are not hiring. That matters for companies whose hiring comes in bursts rather than a steady stream.

## Where it falls short

Reporting is thin. You get pipeline velocity, source of hire and time to fill, but building a custom report or slicing data by department and recruiter is awkward. Talent teams that answer to a board on hiring metrics will outgrow the analytics quickly.

Workable's structured hiring tools are lighter than those of dedicated enterprise systems. Interview kits and scorecards exist, but there is less enforcement of consistency across dozens of hiring managers, and the permissions model is coarse. Companies scaling to hundreds of hires a year with multiple recruiters usually move to [Greenhouse](/tools/greenhouse/), which is built around that kind of process discipline.

The newer HR modules are functional but shallow. If you already run a proper HRIS, you will ignore them, and if you need global payroll or contractor management, a tool like [Deel](/tools/deel/) covers ground Workable does not attempt. The AI sourcing results also vary a great deal by role and location, and are far better for common commercial and technical roles than for niche specialisms.

## Conclusion

Workable is the best fit in this vertical for companies that hire regularly but cannot justify a recruiting operations function. It gets a role live fast, generates candidate volume, and keeps the hiring team organised without much training. Once hiring becomes a core, measured process with several recruiters and demanding reporting needs, its limits show, and it is worth moving to something more structured at that point.
