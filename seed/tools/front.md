## What is Front?

Front is a shared inbox that treats every channel as a conversation in one place. Email is the core, with team and individual inboxes side by side, but the same view holds SMS, WhatsApp, Facebook Messenger, Instagram and live chat from the Front Chat widget. Conversations are assigned, tagged, snoozed and discussed in comments that sit inside the thread, so the internal back and forth about a customer never leaves the customer's record. It started life as a collaboration tool for teams that lived in Gmail, and that DNA still shows: the interface feels like a fast email client with a help desk grafted on rather than the other way round.

Over the last few years Front has grown into a fuller support platform. There is a knowledge base, a rules engine with SLA timers and escalation, macros, CSAT surveys, analytics on response times and team workload, and a set of AI features that summarise threads, draft replies, tag conversations and, with AI Answers, respond to customers directly from knowledge base content. Pricing is per seat with a small minimum team size and no permanent free plan, and the important capabilities such as SLAs, SSO and advanced analytics are spread across the upper tiers.

## Where it shines

Front is unmatched for teams where support, sales and account management all handle the same customers. A logistics coordinator, a customer success manager and a support agent can share one thread, comment on it privately, and hand it over without forwarding anything. The comment and mention model is the best in the category, and it is why so many Front customers are in freight, financial services and B2B software rather than consumer support.

The rules engine is precise. Rules can route by sender domain, account owner, keywords, time of day or SLA breach, and they chain well, so a small operations team can build routing that would take a Zendesk admin considerably longer. Load balancing, round robin and shift schedules are built in.

Integrations are deep where it matters. Salesforce and HubSpot records appear next to the conversation, and the sidebar plugin framework lets internal tools be embedded so agents rarely leave the inbox. Native desktop apps for macOS and Windows, plus good mobile apps, mean the product is fast to work in all day. Analytics cover individual and team performance, tags, SLAs and CSAT in a way that satisfies most managers without needing a data warehouse.

## Where it falls short

Front is not cheap once you need SLAs, analytics and SSO, and the per seat model gets expensive for large agent teams handling simple, high volume consumer support. There is no free plan and the entry tier is limited in team size and features, so most serious deployments land two tiers up.

The support specific depth is thinner than a dedicated help desk. Ticket fields, forms and multi brand help centres are basic, the knowledge base is functional rather than rich, and there is no native voice channel, so phone support means an Aircall or Dialpad integration. AI Answers is newer and less proven than the autonomous agents from the larger vendors.

Teams that need a classic ticket queue with heavy customisation will be better served by [Zendesk](/tools/zendesk/). Ecommerce brands should look at [Gorgias](/tools/gorgias/), and a small team on a budget will get further with [Freshdesk](/tools/freshdesk/) or [Help Scout](/tools/help-scout/).

## Conclusion

Front is the strongest choice when support is a team sport across departments and most of the conversation happens over email and messaging. The collaboration model, rules engine and CRM integrations are excellent, and the desktop apps make it pleasant to live in. If you run a large consumer support operation with phone, forms and strict ticket workflows, a purpose built help desk will fit better and cost less.
