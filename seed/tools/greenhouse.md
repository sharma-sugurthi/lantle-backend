## What is Greenhouse?

Greenhouse is an applicant tracking system built around a specific philosophy: hiring should be structured, consistent and measurable. Every role starts with a kickoff where the team agrees on the attributes that matter, an interview plan assigns each attribute to a specific interview, and interviewers fill in scorecards against those attributes. Decisions are made in a roundup with the evidence laid out, not in a hallway conversation.

Around that core, Greenhouse covers the full recruiting workflow: job posting and syndication, a careers page, a CRM for prospects and events, interview scheduling, offer approvals and an onboarding module sold separately. The integration marketplace is one of the largest in the category, with several hundred partners for assessments, background checks, video interviewing, HRIS syncs and sourcing tools. Reporting is deep, with a report builder, pipeline history and the option to pipe raw data into your own warehouse.

## Where it shines

Consistency is the product. When you have forty hiring managers running interviews, Greenhouse forces the process into a shape that is comparable across roles and teams. Scorecards are hard to skip, interview kits give each interviewer their questions and the attribute they own, and the roundup view makes it obvious when someone is arguing from a gut feeling rather than the evidence collected. Talent leaders who care about reducing bias and being able to defend decisions find this structure valuable.

Reporting is the other big strength. Pipeline conversion by stage, source effectiveness, interviewer load, offer acceptance and time in stage are all available out of the box, and the report builder lets you go further without exporting to a spreadsheet. Companies that report hiring metrics to executives every week rely on this heavily.

The ecosystem matters too. Almost any tool a talent team wants to add already has a Greenhouse integration, and the API is mature enough that internal teams build their own automations on top of it. Compliance features for EEOC reporting, GDPR consent and data retention are solid and well documented.

## Where it falls short

Greenhouse is a lot of system for a small company. Implementation typically takes weeks, requires someone to own the configuration, and the value only appears once the team actually follows the structured process. A ten-person startup hiring three people a year will find it slow and heavy, and will be better served by [Workable](/tools/workable/), which gets a role live in an afternoon.

Pricing is quote-based and sits at the higher end of the market. Contracts are annual, and add-ons such as onboarding and advanced sourcing are priced separately. Budget-conscious teams often feel they are paying for capabilities they use lightly.

The interface, while much improved, still shows its age in places. Some admin tasks take more clicks than they should, candidate-facing scheduling has historically lagged newer competitors, and the built-in sourcing is weaker than dedicated tools, so most customers pair it with a separate sourcing product. Greenhouse also stops at the offer stage: it does not attempt performance management or engagement, which is where [Lattice](/tools/lattice/) picks up.

## Conclusion

Greenhouse is the standard choice for companies that have decided hiring is a discipline rather than a chore. If you are past a hundred employees, have multiple recruiters, and want every hire to go through a defensible, measurable process, it is the most complete option in the category. If you are earlier than that, the structure will feel like friction, and a lighter system will serve you better until you grow into it.
