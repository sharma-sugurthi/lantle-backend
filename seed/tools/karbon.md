## What is Karbon?

Karbon is a practice management platform purpose-built for accounting and bookkeeping firms. It combines a shared email system, task management, client records and recurring work templates into a single workspace. The goal is to replace the combination of shared inboxes, spreadsheet trackers and scattered to-do lists that most firms cobble together as they grow.

Karbon AI adds a layer of intelligence on top of the workflow engine. It can draft replies to client emails, summarize long threads so a colleague can pick up context quickly, and suggest next steps based on the current state of a job. Work templates define the steps for recurring engagements - monthly bookkeeping, quarterly BAS, annual tax returns - and Karbon automatically creates, assigns and schedules those tasks when each period rolls around.

A client portal lets firms request documents, share deliverables and communicate without resorting to separate email chains. Everything ties back to the client record, creating a single source of truth for each engagement.

## Where it shines

The recurring-work engine is Karbon's core strength. Accounting firms live on repeating cycles - the same clients, the same deliverables, the same deadlines, month after month. Karbon turns those cycles into structured templates with clear ownership, due dates and status tracking. Nothing slips through the cracks because a staff member was on leave or forgot to check a spreadsheet.

Email triage inside the platform is a meaningful productivity gain. Instead of switching between an inbox and a project tracker, team members see client emails alongside the related job. Karbon AI's thread summaries provide context in seconds when a partner steps into a conversation a junior staff member has been handling.

AI email drafting saves time on routine correspondence. Requests for missing documents, status updates and follow-ups on overdue items can be generated with a click and reviewed before sending, keeping communication consistent across the firm.

Firm owners and managers can see at a glance which jobs are on track, which are overdue and where bottlenecks are forming - operational transparency that is difficult to achieve with ad-hoc tools.

## Where it falls short

Solo practitioners or very small firms may find Karbon introduces more structure than they need. The setup effort - building templates, migrating contacts, training staff - pays off at scale but can feel heavy for a firm that tracks everything in one spreadsheet.

Migration from an existing system takes real commitment. Data import is not always seamless, and there is a learning curve before the team operates fluently in the new environment.

Karbon does not handle time tracking or billing natively with the depth some firms require, so it often integrates with a separate tool for those functions. Firms wanting a single platform for everything from engagement letters to invoicing may need two or three products.

Per-user costs can add up for larger teams. Firms should model total cost carefully, especially if they also need add-ons for billing or document management.

## Conclusion

Karbon is the leading choice for accounting and bookkeeping firms that want to replace tribal knowledge and scattered tools with a structured, repeatable workflow. The AI features genuinely reduce the time spent on email and status tracking, and the recurring-work engine is exactly what firms need for cyclical engagements. Firms below five people should weigh the setup cost against the benefit, but for practices in the five-to-200-staff range, Karbon brings order to operations in a way that generic project management tools simply cannot match.
