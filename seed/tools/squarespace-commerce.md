## What is Squarespace Commerce?

Squarespace Commerce is the selling layer of the Squarespace website builder. Every Squarespace site is built from a curated set of templates with a section-based editor, and commerce adds product pages, a cart, checkout, order management, inventory, discounts, shipping rates and tax to that foundation. You can sell physical goods, digital downloads, services, gift cards, subscriptions and memberships, and the Acuity scheduling product folds appointment booking into the same account.

Commerce features are tied to the site plan. The lower plans allow selling but charge a transaction fee on top of the processor's rate, and the higher plans remove that fee and add features such as abandoned cart recovery, advanced shipping and the Commerce APIs. Payments go through Squarespace Payments, Stripe or PayPal, and there is a mobile app for editing the site and handling orders.

## Where it shines

The design output is the reason people choose it. Templates are restrained and consistent, typography and spacing are handled well by default, and it is hard to make an ugly store. For photographers, furniture makers, small fashion labels and studios where the product presentation is the pitch, that matters more than a long feature list.

It is easy to run alone. Product setup, discount codes, shipping rules and email campaigns are straightforward, the built-in analytics show sales, traffic and conversion without configuration, and the customer accounts, reviews and waitlists cover the basics of a small catalogue. Selling digital products, courses and memberships works natively rather than through a plugin, which is unusual at this price level.

Squarespace integrates sensibly with the tools a small brand already uses. Products can be synced to Facebook and Instagram shops and to Google Merchant Center, print on demand suppliers connect directly, and the Extensions directory adds shipping, accounting and finance tools without a full app store's overhead.

## Where it falls short

It is not built for scale or complexity. Product variants are capped at a modest number per product, there is no multi-location inventory, no customer group pricing, no wholesale mode and no marketplace listings for Amazon or eBay. Bulk catalogue management is basic and the site search is simple. A store that grows past a few hundred SKUs will feel the ceiling quickly.

The app ecosystem is small by design. The Extensions directory has dozens of tools rather than thousands, so a requirement outside the core, such as personalised recommendations, subscription box logic or complex bundles, may simply not be available. Merchandising is manual, with no automated cross-sell engine and only a related products block.

Checkout cannot be deeply customised, express payment options depend on the processor you choose, and the transaction fee on the lower plans quietly raises the effective cost for a store with real volume. Businesses that expect growth tend to migrate to [Shopify](/tools/shopify/), and those who want more catalogue depth in a hosted package look at [BigCommerce](/tools/bigcommerce/). Site export is limited, so plan for that migration to be a rebuild.

## Conclusion

Squarespace Commerce is a good fit for a small, design-led brand that wants a beautiful site and a simple store it can manage without help. Within that boundary it is pleasant to use and fairly priced. It is a poor fit for large catalogues, wholesale or anyone who expects to need a specialist app, and buyers should choose it knowing that the path to a bigger platform involves starting the storefront again.
