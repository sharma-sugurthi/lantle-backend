## What is Runway?

Runway is a browser-based video generation and editing platform built around its own family of Gen models. The core workflow is simple: write a prompt, or upload a still image, or supply a first and last frame, and the model produces a short clip. You can steer camera movement, apply motion to a selected area with a brush, and extend a clip in stages to build something longer than a single generation allows. Alongside generation, Runway offers a set of AI-assisted editing tools that predate the generative boom: background removal without a green screen, object erasing, frame interpolation for slow motion, and colour grading.

Act-One takes a webcam recording of a person speaking and transfers the performance onto a generated or illustrated character, which makes it usable for explainer content and animated presenters. More recent models accept an existing video and a text instruction and return an edited version, so you can change a setting, relight a scene or remove an element without keyframing anything. Pricing is credit based. A free tier gives a small allowance to experiment with, and paid plans add monthly credits, higher resolution exports, watermark removal and longer clip durations. An API is available for teams that want to build generation into their own products.

## Where it shines

Image-to-video is the most dependable workflow. Give Runway a well-composed still, whether a product photograph or an illustration, and ask for subtle camera movement, and the results are usable for social and web with very little iteration. Agencies use this to bring static campaign assets to life without a second shoot.

The editing tools are genuinely practical. Rotoscoping that used to take an afternoon happens in a few clicks, and the inpainting handles removing a logo or a stray passer-by cleanly enough for most delivery formats. For a small video team, these features alone can justify a subscription before any generation happens.

Act-One fills a real gap. Being able to record a script once and drive several different characters with the same take is a workflow that did not exist before, and the lip sync and expression transfer hold up well on medium shots.

Runway also behaves like a mature product rather than a research demo. Projects, asset libraries, version history and team workspaces are all present, and the interface is familiar to anyone who has used a timeline editor.

## Where it falls short

Clips are short, and stitching them into something coherent is still manual work. Characters drift between generations, lighting shifts, and physics can go wrong in ways that are obvious to any viewer. Producing a thirty second piece that holds together typically means generating far more footage than you keep, and the credits go quickly. Budget for waste.

Text prompts alone are the least reliable input. Complex scenes with several actors, specific choreography or readable on-screen text often need many attempts. Teams that want a consistent visual world are better served by generating stills in [Midjourney](/tools/midjourney/) first and animating the ones that work.

Audio is not part of the pipeline. Generated clips arrive silent, so voiceover, music and sound design happen elsewhere. [ElevenLabs](/tools/elevenlabs/) covers narration and sound effects and is the natural pairing. Rendering can also be slow at busy times on the lower plans, which is worth knowing before a deadline.

## Conclusion

Runway is the most complete video tool in this category for teams that need short generated or AI-edited clips and want a proper production environment around the model. It is not a replacement for a shoot or an editor, and the credit economics punish undisciplined experimentation. Used for image-to-video, quick edits and performance transfer, it earns its place in a modern content workflow.
