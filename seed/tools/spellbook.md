## What is Spellbook?

Spellbook is an AI drafting and review assistant that runs as an add-in inside Microsoft Word. It was built by Rally, a legal technology company, and it works directly on the document a lawyer already has open rather than asking anyone to upload files to a separate portal. Highlight a clause and ask it to tighten the language, flag risks in a counterparty draft, suggest missing provisions, or generate a first pass at a new section based on the rest of the agreement.

The review mode reads the whole contract and produces a list of issues with suggested redlines, which can be accepted as tracked changes in Word. Playbooks let a firm or in house team encode its own positions, so review runs against your standard fallbacks rather than a generic notion of market terms. Benchmarks compare individual clauses against what Spellbook considers typical for that document type. Spellbook Associate, the agent layer, takes a multi-step instruction such as redrafting an agreement to reflect a term sheet and works through the document on its own, leaving a lawyer to check the result.

## Where it shines

The Word integration is the whole point and it is done well. There is no context switching, no copying text into a chat window, and the output lands as tracked changes the lawyer can accept or reject clause by clause. For anyone who drafts every day, that saves real time compared with general purpose chat tools.

Review quality on standard commercial paper is good. NDAs, service agreements, licences and employment contracts are where the model has seen the most examples, and it reliably catches missing limitation of liability caps, one sided indemnities and undefined terms. It is a strong second set of eyes at the first read stage, especially for junior lawyers and for solo practitioners who have nobody down the hall to ask.

Setup is quick. Install the add-in, sign in, and start using it on the next matter. Pricing is per seat on a subscription and there is a trial, so a small firm can evaluate it on live work without a procurement cycle. Spellbook publishes its security posture, holds SOC 2 certification and states that customer documents are not used to train the underlying models, which matters when the documents are privileged.

## Where it falls short

Spellbook is a drafting tool, not a contract management system. It has no repository, no intake workflow, no approval routing and no obligations tracking. Teams that need to know what was signed, when it renews and who approved it will need something else alongside it. [Ironclad](/tools/ironclad/) covers that entire lifecycle, at a very different price and implementation effort.

It only works in Word. Firms on Google Docs, or lawyers who negotiate in PDF, do not get the core experience. Output on unusual or highly specialised agreements is less reliable, and the model will occasionally propose language that is confidently wrong. Every suggestion still needs a qualified lawyer to review before it goes to the other side; Spellbook does not give legal advice and should not be treated as if it does.

The per seat cost is easy to justify for people who draft daily and harder for occasional users, and larger teams may find the seat count adds up faster than expected.

## Conclusion

Spellbook is the best AI assistant for lawyers who do their contract work in Microsoft Word. It fits into an existing workflow rather than replacing it, the review output is useful on everyday commercial agreements, and a small team can be productive with it within a day. It is not a lifecycle system and it is not a substitute for judgement, but for drafting and first pass review it is the tool we would pick.
