## What is Motion?

Motion is not a scheduling link product that added extras. It is a calendar and task manager where the scheduling is done by an algorithm. You enter tasks with a deadline, a duration and a priority, and Motion places them into the free space on your calendar around your meetings. When a meeting moves, a task overruns or something urgent arrives, the plan is rebuilt. The calendar you look at each morning is the plan for the day, and it was made for you rather than by you.

Around that core sit the features you would expect from a scheduling tool. Booking pages let people reserve time with you, with availability windows, buffers, and the option to have Motion protect focus time from being booked over. There is a meeting assistant that drafts agendas, and a project management layer with tasks, statuses, assignees and templates so a small team can run its work in the same place the work is scheduled. Calendars sync with Google, Outlook and iCloud, video links come from Zoom, Meet and Teams, and there are desktop and mobile apps alongside the web version.

## Where it shines

Automatic planning is genuinely different from anything else in the category. For someone who juggles many small commitments and consistently underestimates how much fits in a week, seeing tasks scheduled against real time is clarifying. Deadlines that cannot be met surface early, and the daily plan adjusts without you touching it.

The combination of tasks, projects and calendar in one tool removes a class of tool switching. A freelancer or founder can hold client work, internal projects and meetings in one view, and the booking page respects the task schedule so external meetings do not silently wipe out a delivery.

Focus time protection is practical. Motion blocks deep work as a calendar event so colleagues see you as busy, moves it when necessary, and prevents booking links from landing meetings on top of it.

For small teams the workload view is useful. A manager can see whether an assignee has room for a new task before the deadline, based on their actual calendar rather than a guess.

## Where it falls short

As a pure scheduling link product Motion is weaker than the specialists. Booking pages lack the routing forms, weighted round robin and CRM lookup that [Calendly](/tools/calendly/) provides for sales teams, and the invitee experience is plainer than [SavvyCal](/tools/savvycal/). If external booking is your main need, Motion is not the right buy.

The algorithm needs trust and discipline. It only works if every task carries a realistic duration and deadline, and if you actually do the task when it is scheduled. People who keep a loose list of intentions rather than estimated work find the constant rescheduling more noise than help, and turning the automation off leaves an ordinary task manager.

Pricing is per user and there is no free plan, only a short trial. Compared with a free scheduling link plus a free task manager, the monthly cost is meaningful for an individual.

The product has grown fast and the interface reflects it: many features, some overlapping, and occasional bugs with calendar sync or tasks that reschedule unexpectedly. Large organisations will find the admin controls, permissions and reporting thin compared with established project tools.

## Conclusion

Motion is the right choice when the problem is not finding a slot for other people but finding time for your own work. The automatic planning, focus protection and project tracking in one calendar suit busy individuals and small teams who want the day organised for them. Treat the booking pages as a convenience rather than the reason to buy, and choose a dedicated scheduling tool if inbound meetings are what you need to manage.
