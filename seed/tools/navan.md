## What is Navan?

Navan combines corporate travel booking with expense management and corporate cards in a single platform. Because the system knows the trip details - flights, hotels, ground transportation - it can automatically match charges and receipts to the correct trip, enforce travel policy at the moment of booking and reconcile everything without requiring the traveller to file a traditional expense report.

When plans change, Navan handles rebooking, cancellations and refund tracking automatically. The AI monitors price drops after booking and can rebook at a lower fare, splitting the savings between the company and the traveller as an incentive. Corporate cards issued through the platform feed transactions directly into the expense engine, closing the loop between spending and reporting.

The platform integrates with major accounting systems, HRIS tools and single sign-on providers, so it fits into existing enterprise infrastructure without requiring a parallel tech stack.

## Where it shines

The genuine integration of travel and expenses is Navan's defining advantage. Most companies treat these as separate workflows - one tool for booking flights and hotels, another for submitting receipts afterward. Navan collapses them into a single flow. Because it already knows what the trip cost and where the traveller stayed, matching a hotel receipt to the right booking happens without any manual effort.

Policy enforcement at booking time is a meaningful upgrade over after-the-fact auditing. Instead of approving an expense report and discovering that someone booked a first-class ticket, Navan can present only in-policy options or require pre-approval before an out-of-policy selection is confirmed. This prevents overspend rather than simply catching it.

The price-drop rebooking feature is a genuine cost saver. For companies with significant travel volume, the cumulative savings from automatic fare monitoring add up quickly. Sharing a portion of the savings with the traveller creates an incentive to book through the platform rather than going around it.

Navan offers a free tier for small companies, which lowers the barrier for startups and early-stage teams that travel occasionally but do not yet need enterprise-grade procurement. This makes it accessible well before a company reaches the scale where travel management typically becomes a priority.

## Where it falls short

Companies where employees rarely travel will find much of the platform unused. The travel engine is the heart of Navan, and without meaningful travel volume, the expense management and card features alone may not justify choosing it over a dedicated spend-management tool. Teams in that situation should consider [Brex](/tools/brex/) for cards with international capabilities or [Expensify](/tools/expensify/) for a straightforward expense-report workflow.

Some frequent travellers prefer booking directly with airlines and hotels to maintain loyalty programme status. Navan supports many loyalty programmes, but the experience is not always identical to booking direct, and travellers with strong brand preferences sometimes resist using an intermediary.

Reporting and analytics are not yet as customizable as dedicated business-intelligence tools. Finance teams that want highly tailored dashboards may need to export data and build reports elsewhere.

International coverage is broad but uneven. Major routes are well served, but less common destinations or regional carriers may have limited inventory.

## Conclusion

Navan is the strongest option for companies where travel represents a major spending category and the goal is to control costs proactively rather than reconcile them after the fact. The integration of booking, policy, cards and expenses into one flow eliminates friction for travellers and gives finance teams real-time visibility. Companies with minimal travel needs will get more value from a dedicated card or expense platform, but for travel-heavy organizations, Navan delivers measurable savings and a noticeably better employee experience.
