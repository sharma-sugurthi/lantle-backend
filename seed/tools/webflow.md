## What is Webflow?

Webflow is a visual development tool. Designers build pages by manipulating real HTML and CSS through a visual interface, with a CMS for structured content and hosting included, and the result is production code rather than a template with settings. Ecommerce is one of the site plan types: turning it on adds products, categories, a cart, checkout, order management, shipping and tax settings, and customer accounts to the same designer.

The free Starter plan lets you build and preview on a Webflow subdomain, which is why the product counts as freemium, but selling requires a paid ecommerce plan on a custom domain. The ecommerce tiers differ in transaction fee, item limits, staff seats and sales volume allowances. Payments run through Stripe, with PayPal, Apple Pay and Google Pay available at checkout, and a Marketplace of templates and apps has grown up around the platform.

## Where it shines

Nothing else in this category gives you this much control over how the store looks. Product pages, category grids, the cart and even the checkout layout are designed element by element with the same tools used for the rest of the site, and animations and interactions carry through. For brands whose site is a piece of the product, that is worth a great deal.

The CMS makes content-led selling natural. A lookbook, a story about a maker and the products it features can be modelled as related collections and rendered together, with the CMS and Ecommerce APIs available for syncing data in from a PIM or out to a warehouse. Hosting is fast and managed, with a global CDN, SSL and backups handled, so there is no server to maintain as there would be on WordPress.

Webflow is also a strong choice when the store is secondary. Agencies and startups that mainly need a marketing site with a merchandise shop or a small product line get one tool, one bill and one design system, and the Localization and Optimize add-ons cover multilingual sites and A/B testing without a separate stack.

## Where it falls short

The ecommerce feature set has moved slowly for years and it shows. There is no native subscription selling, no multi-currency checkout, no customer group pricing, limited discount rule logic, and item counts are capped per plan. Inventory is single location and there are no marketplace listings. Compared with a dedicated platform, order management is thin and reporting is basic.

The app ecosystem for commerce is small. Reviews, loyalty, returns and personalised recommendations mostly rely on embedding third-party scripts rather than integrated apps, and the checkout, while stylable, cannot run custom logic. Expect to lean on external tools for anything beyond a simple purchase flow.

Cost and learning curve are both higher than a store builder. Designing well in Webflow requires understanding the box model and CSS concepts, and the ecommerce plans cost more than the equivalent Shopify tiers once volume allowances are considered. A brand whose business is the store, not the site, will be better served by [Shopify](/tools/shopify/) or, for a large catalogue with hosted convenience, [BigCommerce](/tools/bigcommerce/).

## Conclusion

Webflow is the right pick when design control and a custom marketing site are the priority and the shop is a small, well-defined catalogue. It produces storefronts that look like nothing else and keeps design and content in one place. As a commerce platform for a growing product business it is behind the specialists, and buyers should choose it for the site first and the store second.
