## What is Fireflies?

Fireflies is a meeting notetaker built for teams that want the output of every call to land somewhere useful. Its bot, Fred, joins calls on Zoom, Google Meet, Microsoft Teams and several other platforms, records the audio, and produces a transcript with speaker labels, a summary and a list of action items. The notes are then routed onward through integrations: deal notes into Salesforce or HubSpot, tasks into Asana or Trello, summaries into Slack or Notion.

Beyond notes, Fireflies stores every recording in a searchable library. AskFred, the assistant, can answer questions about a single call or across many, and the topic tracker lets you define keywords such as pricing, competitor names or objections and see where they appear across all conversations. Conversation intelligence dashboards report on talk time, sentiment, question counts and filler words per person. There is a free plan with limited storage and summaries, and paid tiers add unlimited transcription, more storage, video recording and admin controls.

## Where it shines

The integration catalogue is the main differentiator. Fireflies connects to a long list of CRMs, project managers, dialers and storage tools, and the CRM syncs are more than a paste of the summary: they can attach notes to the right contact and deal, log the meeting as an activity and update fields. For a sales or customer success team this removes the post-call admin that nobody does properly.

Search across the whole meeting archive is fast and works well. Being able to filter every call by speaker, date, topic or keyword, then jump to the exact moment in the recording, is useful for onboarding new reps, settling disputes about what a customer agreed to, and preparing for renewals. Language support is broad, with dozens of transcription languages, which matters for international teams.

The summary formats are configurable. You can set up templates so that a sales call produces a different structure from a stand-up or a user interview, and custom vocabulary improves recognition of product names and jargon. The API and webhooks make it possible to feed transcripts into internal systems for teams with a developer to spare.

## Where it falls short

Transcript accuracy is good but not best in class on noisy or accented audio, and speaker attribution occasionally drifts on calls with more than four participants. Summaries can be generic on unstructured conversations, and action items sometimes include things that were merely discussed rather than agreed. Someone still needs to read the notes before forwarding them to a customer.

The bot-based approach has the same social cost as its competitors. A named attendee called Fred appears in every meeting, external participants ask what it is, and some clients decline to be recorded. Default settings can send recap emails to everyone on the invite, which surprises people. There is a desktop app that records without a bot, but it is newer and less proven than the bot workflow.

Some users find the interface busy, with many features surfaced at once, and the free plan's limits on AI summaries are tight enough that it works mainly as a trial. For an individual who just wants clean transcripts and a summary at the lowest price, [Otter](/tools/otter/) is simpler and cheaper. Teams already living inside [Notion](/tools/notion/) may find Notion's built in meeting notes adequate for internal calls and keep Fireflies for customer facing ones.

## Conclusion

Fireflies is the right choice when the point of recording a meeting is to update other systems. The CRM and project tool integrations, the cross-meeting search and the wide language support make it the most useful notetaker for revenue and customer teams. If you only need transcripts for yourself, it is more than you need. If your CRM is the system of record, Fireflies is the one to pick.
