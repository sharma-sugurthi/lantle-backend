## What is Deel?

Deel is a global payroll and compliance platform. Its original product is the employer of record service: Deel has legal entities in a large number of countries and hires people on your behalf where you have no entity, handling the local contract, payroll, benefits, taxes and statutory filings. Alongside that sits contractor management, where you onboard independent contractors with locally compliant agreements, collect tax forms and pay them in their preferred currency or method from a single invoice.

Over the past few years Deel has expanded into global payroll for companies that do have their own entities, a free HRIS for employee records, time off and org charts, immigration and visa support, equipment provisioning, and background checks. The contract generation, onboarding flows and payments are all in one interface, and the compliance team behind it keeps local templates up to date as employment law changes.

## Where it shines

Speed of international hiring is the reason most companies sign up. If you find a strong candidate in a country where you have no presence, Deel can have a compliant contract issued and the person onboarded in days rather than the months it takes to set up an entity. For distributed teams that hire wherever talent is, this removes a whole category of legal and administrative work.

Contractor payments are handled well. Contractors submit invoices or are paid on a fixed schedule, you approve in bulk, and Deel handles currency conversion, local payment rails and the paperwork that keeps the relationship classified correctly. The misclassification risk assessment is a useful nudge for companies that are not sure whether someone should be a contractor or an employee.

The free HRIS is a genuinely reasonable starting point. Employee records, documents, time off and org charts are enough for a young company, and because the paid payroll and employer of record products plug into it, you avoid duplicate data entry as you grow. Coverage across countries is among the widest available, and the in-app support for local questions is responsive.

## Where it falls short

Employer of record pricing is per employee per month and is material at scale. Once you have more than a handful of people in one country, setting up your own entity and moving them to Deel's own-entity payroll is usually cheaper, and Deel will help with that transition, but it is a cost to plan for. Contractor fees and currency spreads also add up for companies paying large numbers of people.

Benefits under the employer of record model are Deel's standardised packages, not ones you design. In some countries that is fine, but in others employees notice the difference from what a local employer would offer, and you have limited room to customise. Some customers also report that support quality varies by country, with the more established markets served better than newer ones.

Deel is not a recruiting or performance tool. There is no applicant tracking, so you still need something like [Workable](/tools/workable/) to find people, and its performance features are far lighter than a dedicated platform such as [Lattice](/tools/lattice/). The breadth of the product also means some newer modules feel less mature than the core payroll and contractor flows.

## Conclusion

Deel is the strongest option for companies that pay people in several countries and do not want to become experts in each one's employment law. The employer of record and contractor products are mature, the country coverage is wide, and the free HRIS lowers the barrier to starting. Watch the per-head cost as teams in any single country grow, and do not expect it to replace your recruiting or performance tools.
