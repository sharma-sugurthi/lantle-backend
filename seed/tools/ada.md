## What is Ada?

Ada is an AI customer service agent aimed at enterprises with support volumes measured in millions of conversations. It started as a no-code chatbot builder and has rebuilt itself around a generative AI agent that reads your knowledge sources, reasons about the customer's question, calls your backend systems to look up or change data, and resolves the conversation without a human. It runs on web chat, in mobile apps, over email, on WhatsApp, Messenger and SMS, and, more recently, on voice.

Ada does not try to be the help desk. It sits in front of Zendesk, Salesforce Service Cloud, Freshdesk, Kustomer or a contact centre platform such as Genesys or Talkdesk, resolves what it can, and hands the rest over with context. Pricing is quote based and typically tied to volume or automated resolutions rather than seats, which is why it lands in the enterprise bracket.

## Where it shines

Resolution rate on real, messy traffic is Ada's strength. The reasoning engine handles multi-part questions, follows up when the customer is vague, and is measured on resolved conversations rather than deflected ones. Customers in fintech, telecoms, ecommerce and travel routinely report the majority of inbound volume being handled end to end, and Ada's reporting is built around proving that number.

Actions are the second reason to buy it. Through API connections Ada can check an order, reissue a card, change a booking or update an address inside the conversation, with guardrails that define what the agent may and may not do. A playbook layer lets support leaders describe procedures in plain language, so business teams can change agent behaviour without waiting for engineering.

Coverage is broad. The agent works in dozens of languages from one knowledge base, and the same configuration is reused across chat, email and voice, so a company does not maintain three separate bots. The voice product is newer but the ability to run one policy across every channel is genuinely useful for large operations.

Governance is mature, with role-based access, audit logs, SSO, data residency options and testing tooling that lets teams simulate conversations before shipping a change. This is the kind of rigour large regulated companies need and that most chatbot vendors lack.

## Where it falls short

Ada is not for small teams. Contracts are annual, sales led and sized for companies with a dedicated support operations function. Implementation takes weeks and a knowledgeable owner on the customer side, and the value only appears at volumes where a high automation rate saves real headcount. A startup wanting a bot on its site should look at [Crisp](/tools/crisp/) or [Tidio](/tools/tidio/) instead.

Because Ada is not a help desk, you still need one. Human agents work in Zendesk or Salesforce, and the handoff quality depends on how well that integration is configured. Companies that would rather buy one platform for both AI and human support will find [Intercom](/tools/intercom/) simpler, since Fin and the inbox come from the same vendor.

Pricing is opaque until you are deep in a sales cycle, and resolution-based models require careful definition of what counts as resolved. Ask for that definition in writing.

## Conclusion

Ada is one of the two or three AI agents an enterprise support leader should evaluate seriously. It resolves a large share of volume, performs real actions, runs across every channel and comes with the governance a regulated company expects. It is expensive, sales led and dependent on a separate help desk, so the fit is large brands with high volume, not growing teams looking for a chat widget.
