## What is Gong?

Gong records and transcribes sales calls from Zoom, Teams, Google Meet and dialers, captures the emails and calendar events around each deal, and turns all of it into a searchable record of what is actually happening with customers. Every call gets a transcript, speaker separation, topic detection and an AI summary with next steps. Managers can search across thousands of calls for a competitor name, a pricing objection or a specific phrase and jump to the moment it was said.

On top of the recordings sits a deal and forecasting layer. Deal boards show which opportunities have gone quiet, which are missing a decision maker on recent calls and which have had no activity since the last stage change, all inferred from captured conversations rather than what a rep typed into the CRM. Forecasting rolls those signals into a projection alongside rep submitted numbers. Gong also sells engagement tools for sequencing and dialing, though most customers still buy it primarily for recording, coaching and deal visibility. Pricing is quoted per seat plus a platform fee through a sales process rather than published online.

## Where it shines

Coaching is where Gong earns its cost. New reps can watch curated libraries of the best discovery calls, managers can leave time stamped comments on a recording, and scorecards standardise what good looks like across a team. Talk ratio, longest monologue and question rate are blunt instruments on their own, but combined with regular call review they shorten ramp noticeably.

The deal inspection view catches problems earlier than pipeline reviews do. Seeing that the champion has not been on a call in three weeks, or that pricing was never discussed on a deal marked as late stage, gives managers something concrete to ask about. The AI generated account summaries are good enough that a leader can prepare for an executive call without reading a full email thread.

Search across the corpus is valuable well beyond sales. Product teams find every mention of a requested feature, marketing hears the language customers use and competitive teams track how rivals are positioned in real deals. Transcription quality in English is high, and support for other major languages has improved steadily.

Integrations with Salesforce and HubSpot are mature, and the data Gong writes back, such as auto captured contacts and activity, improves CRM hygiene without rep effort.

## Where it falls short

Cost is the obvious barrier. Gong is priced for larger teams and the platform fee makes it hard to justify below roughly twenty seats. Small teams get most of the recording and summarising benefit from the native call features in [HubSpot Sales Hub](/tools/hubspot-sales-hub/) or a cheaper standalone note taker, and should not buy Gong until they have managers with time to coach.

The value depends entirely on management follow through. Companies that buy Gong and then never review calls end up with an expensive archive. The metrics can also encourage gaming, where reps optimise talk ratio rather than outcomes, so scorecards need thought before they are rolled out.

Recording raises consent and privacy questions, particularly in Europe and in regulated industries, and some prospects decline to be recorded. Gong handles consent notices and offers regional data storage, but legal review is part of any rollout. The engagement tools are weaker than dedicated outbound products, and teams running prospecting at volume still pair Gong with [Apollo](/tools/apollo/) or a similar sequencer rather than consolidating.

## Conclusion

Gong remains the reference product in conversation intelligence, and for sales organisations with enough reps and managers who will actually coach, the improvement in ramp time, deal visibility and forecast accuracy justifies the spend. It is the wrong purchase for small teams, for organisations without a coaching culture, or for anyone hoping the software will fix a pipeline problem on its own. Buy it when you have the people to use it.
