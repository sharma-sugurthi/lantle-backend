## What is Ironclad?

Ironclad is a contract lifecycle management platform built around a workflow engine. Legal teams design intake forms and approval paths in Workflow Designer, a drag and drop builder that needs no code, and the rest of the business requests NDAs, sales agreements, vendor contracts and offer letters through those forms. Ironclad generates the document from an approved template, routes it for review, tracks negotiation in its own browser based editor or in Microsoft Word, sends it for signature through DocuSign or Adobe Acrobat Sign, and files the executed copy in a searchable repository.

AI sits across several stages. Smart Import reads legacy agreements and extracts parties, dates, renewal terms and key clauses into structured metadata. AI Assist suggests redlines against a playbook while a lawyer reviews counterparty paper, and drafts fallback language on request. The repository lets you ask questions across the whole contract base in plain language, which is useful when someone wants to know how many agreements contain a particular indemnity or which renewals land next quarter. Clickwrap, which came from the PactSafe acquisition, handles online terms acceptance and keeps an audit trail of who agreed to what.

## Where it shines

The workflow engine is the reason to buy Ironclad. Once intake is defined, legal stops being a mailbox. Sales reps launch their own NDAs from Salesforce, procurement kicks off vendor reviews from Coupa, and every request arrives with the right fields already populated. Conditional logic sends a standard NDA straight to signature while anything with non-standard terms goes to a human. Teams that previously tracked contracts in a shared drive and a spreadsheet report a visible drop in turnaround time.

The repository is useful rather than a filing cabinet. Because metadata is extracted on import and captured during workflow, reporting on obligations, renewal dates and clause variance works from day one. Finance and sales operations teams end up using it as much as legal does.

Integrations are broad. Salesforce, Slack, Workday, Coupa and the major signature providers all have first party connectors, and the API is documented well enough for engineering teams to build custom triggers. Negotiating in the Ironclad Editor with tracked changes keeps version history inside the system, so the final signed document can be traced back through every turn.

## Where it falls short

Ironclad is an enterprise purchase. Pricing is quote based, contracts are annual, and implementation is a project measured in months rather than days. Someone on the legal or operations side has to own the build out of workflows and templates, and that person needs time and a mandate. Small teams that mainly want faster redlining will find the platform heavy for the job.

The AI review is tied to Ironclad's own environment. Lawyers who prefer to stay in Word for the whole negotiation get a thinner experience than in the native editor, and the AI suggestions still need a lawyer's judgement on every clause. Ironclad does not give legal advice and does not claim to; it speeds up review that a qualified person still has to sign off on.

For a solo lawyer or a small in house team that lives in Word and just wants AI review and drafting help, [Spellbook](/tools/spellbook/) is far cheaper and far quicker to adopt. If the main problem is getting documents signed and stored rather than negotiated, [DocuSign](/tools/docusign/) covers that with less setup.

## Conclusion

Ironclad is the right choice for legal teams at mid-size and large companies that process a high volume of contracts and want a single system for intake, negotiation, signature and storage. The workflow builder and repository are the strongest in the category, and the AI features are useful additions rather than the core reason to buy. Budget for a serious implementation, and do not choose it if your real need is lightweight contract review.
