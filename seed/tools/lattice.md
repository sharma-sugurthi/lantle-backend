## What is Lattice?

Lattice is a people management platform that started with performance reviews and grew outward. The core modules are performance (review cycles, 360 feedback, calibration), goals and OKRs, one-on-one agendas with shared notes and action items, continuous feedback and praise, engagement surveys with pulse follow-ups, and career development through competency matrices and growth plans. Compensation review cycles and a basic HRIS have been added more recently, so a smaller company can run its employee records, reviews and pay adjustments in the same place.

The product is designed so that these pieces feed each other. A goal set at the start of a quarter shows up in the one-on-one agenda, feedback given through the year is pulled into the review packet, and the review outcome flows into the compensation cycle. Managers do most of their work in a single home view or inside Slack and Microsoft Teams, where reminders and quick feedback prompts land.

## Where it shines

Review cycles are where Lattice is strongest. Building a cycle with self, manager, peer and upward components is straightforward, the question templates are sensible, and the calibration view lets leaders compare ratings across teams before results are released. HR teams that previously ran reviews in forms and spreadsheets typically cut the administrative effort by a large margin and get far higher completion rates.

The one-on-one tool is genuinely used by managers, which is rarer than it sounds. Shared agendas, carried-over action items and the ability to attach goals and recent feedback make the meeting more useful, and because it lives in Slack, adoption does not depend on people remembering to open another app.

Engagement surveys are well designed. The question bank is grounded in organisational psychology, benchmarks are available for comparison, and the results view highlights drivers of engagement rather than just showing a score. Manager-level dashboards with anonymity thresholds mean you can hand results to team leads without a data privacy problem. Goals and OKRs are simple enough that non-technical teams actually maintain them.

## Where it falls short

Lattice is priced per employee per month, with each module sold separately or bundled. The cost adds up quickly once you turn on performance, engagement, growth and compensation for a few hundred people, and many customers end up paying for modules they use lightly. Small teams that only need reviews may find it hard to justify.

The HRIS is young. It handles employee records, time off and org charts adequately, but companies with complex payroll, benefits administration or multi-country requirements will still need a dedicated system. If your workforce spans several countries, [Deel](/tools/deel/) covers global payroll and compliance in a way Lattice does not try to.

Lattice also does nothing before the hire. There is no applicant tracking or sourcing, so it sits downstream of a system like [Greenhouse](/tools/greenhouse/) rather than replacing it. Some users find the goals module too flexible, with no strong enforcement of cadence or alignment, and reporting across modules can require exports to answer non-standard questions. The compensation module is newer and less polished than the performance tools.

## Conclusion

Lattice is the most complete option for a people team that wants reviews, goals, one-on-ones and engagement in a single, well-connected system. It rewards companies that commit to the whole platform and have someone in HR to drive the cadence. Companies that only want a simple review tool, or that need a serious HRIS first, should think carefully about whether the per-module pricing is worth it.
