## What is Kayako?

Kayako is one of the older names in help desk software. It began as a downloadable ticketing system that companies ran on their own servers, became a cloud product with a modern shared inbox in the mid 2010s, and has since been folded into a larger software group that has repositioned it around AI. Today it is sold as a cloud help desk with tickets, SLAs, live chat, a help centre and an AI layer that drafts and, where you allow it, sends replies based on past tickets and articles. The legacy on-premise edition is still maintained for customers who need to host their own data.

The product's distinctive idea has always been the customer journey timeline. A conversation shows not just the ticket but what the customer did before and after: pages viewed, previous tickets, purchases and events pushed in from other systems. Agents get context without clicking into a CRM, and the help centre and chat widget feed the same record. Pricing is per agent and, since the relaunch, is generally quoted rather than listed, with the AI features positioned as the main reason to buy.

## Where it shines

The timeline is still useful. A support agent can see that a customer opened the pricing page twice, read a billing article and then wrote in, which changes the reply. Combined with the SingleView integration layer, events from a product or billing system appear inline, and that is something the cheaper tools do not offer without custom work.

Core ticketing is solid. SLAs with business hours, escalation rules, macros, collision detection, custom fields and multilingual help centres cover what a traditional support team needs. Kayako's automation runs on triggers and monitors, so routing and reminders can be configured by an administrator without a developer.

The AI drafting does the job it claims. Trained on your resolved tickets and help centre, it produces first drafts that are often close to what an experienced agent would write, and the review before send workflow makes it safe to adopt gradually. For a team with a large archive of good answers, this is a fast way to bring new agents up to speed.

Self-hosting remains an option through the classic edition, which matters for a small set of regulated companies that cannot put support data in a multi tenant cloud. Very few help desks still offer that path.

## Where it falls short

The ownership changes have cost the product momentum. Development pace slowed for years, the integration catalogue is small compared with the leaders, and the community around it is thin, so answers to configuration questions are harder to find. Pricing is no longer published, so budgeting needs a sales conversation before you can compare it fairly with tools that list their plans.

Channel coverage lags. Email, chat, Facebook and Twitter are supported, but there is no native voice and no WhatsApp, and the chat widget looks dated next to modern messengers. The reporting is adequate for a manager but nowhere near Zendesk Explore, and the mobile apps are basic. The relaunch has focused on autonomous AI claims that are hard to verify without a trial.

For a mainstream help desk with a large ecosystem, [Zendesk](/tools/zendesk/) is the safer bet. Teams that want a low cost ticketing system with SLAs should look at [Freshdesk](/tools/freshdesk/), and a company wanting support inside its CRM will get more from [HubSpot Service Hub](/tools/hubspot-service-hub/).

## Conclusion

Kayako makes sense for two kinds of buyer: a company that needs self-hosted ticketing with SLAs and a proper help centre, and an existing customer with years of ticket history who wants AI drafting trained on it. For everyone else, the uncertainty around product direction and pricing outweighs the timeline feature, and a more actively developed help desk is the lower risk choice.
