## What is Wix eCommerce?

Wix eCommerce is the store layer of the Wix website builder. You design pages with a freeform drag and drop editor, or let the AI site generator draft a layout from a description, then add Wix Stores to sell physical products, digital downloads, services or subscriptions from the same dashboard. Payments run through Wix Payments or a connected gateway, and orders, inventory, shipping labels, discounts and abandoned cart emails are all managed inside the Wix admin.

The commerce features require one of the business-oriented site plans. A free Wix site exists, but it carries Wix branding and cannot take payments, so in practice a store means a paid subscription from day one. Wix also sells point of sale hardware, a mobile owner app for managing orders on the go, and a set of marketing tools for email, SMS and social posting that plug into the store data.

## Where it shines

Design freedom is the draw. Unlike template-driven store builders where you rearrange sections, Wix lets you place elements anywhere on the canvas, which suits small brands whose identity matters more than a conventional product grid. The template library is large and the newer Studio editor gives agencies responsive controls that were missing from the classic editor.

Multichannel selling is built in. Product listings can be pushed to Amazon, eBay, Facebook, Instagram and Google Shopping from the dashboard, with inventory kept in sync across channels. For a small catalogue, this is a lot less setup than it would be on a self-hosted store.

The all-in-one dashboard is genuinely convenient for a one-person business. Bookings, events, restaurant ordering, a blog, email campaigns, invoicing and the store share one login and one customer list, and the Wix App Market covers loyalty, reviews, print on demand and dropshipping suppliers with a couple of clicks. The Velo development platform adds JavaScript, a database and REST APIs when you outgrow the point and click tools.

## Where it falls short

Wix is a website builder first and a commerce platform second. The product model handles variants and options but strains with large or highly attributed catalogues, and filtering and search on the storefront are simpler than on dedicated platforms. Bulk operations on thousands of SKUs are slow, and there is no equivalent of the customer group pricing or B2B features that mid-market sellers rely on.

Portability is poor. A Wix site cannot be exported to another host, so leaving means rebuilding the storefront elsewhere, with only the product and customer data coming along. Site performance has improved but a busy freeform design still tends to load more slowly than a lean theme on a specialist platform.

Checkout customisation is limited and merchandising automation is thin. Related products and simple upsell modules exist, but personalised recommendations of the kind [Rebuy](/tools/rebuy/) provides on Shopify have no real equivalent. Sellers who expect to grow past a small catalogue usually end up on [Shopify](/tools/shopify/) or, if they value data ownership, [WooCommerce](/tools/woocommerce/).

## Conclusion

Wix eCommerce is a sensible choice for a small business that wants an attractive website with selling as one of several jobs it does. The editor, the bundled marketing tools and the multichannel listings cover a lot for the money. It is not the right foundation for a store with a large catalogue or ambitions to scale into wholesale, and the lack of an exit path should weigh on that decision.
