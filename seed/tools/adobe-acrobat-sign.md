## What is Adobe Acrobat Sign?

Adobe Acrobat Sign is Adobe's e-signature service, sold on its own and bundled into Acrobat Pro for individuals and teams. The sending flow is familiar: upload a file, add signature and form fields, set the signing order and send. Recipients sign in a browser or the Acrobat mobile app without creating an account, and the completed PDF comes back with an audit report. Signatures meet ESIGN and UETA, and Adobe has spent years on compliance work, so eIDAS advanced and qualified electronic signatures, HIPAA configurations, FedRAMP and 21 CFR Part 11 for regulated industries are all covered on the enterprise editions.

What distinguishes it from other signature tools is how deeply it sits inside the Adobe and Microsoft ecosystems. Acrobat users can request signatures from the same window they use to edit and comment on PDFs. Inside Microsoft 365, signing is available from Word, Outlook, Teams, SharePoint, Dynamics and Power Automate. There are also native connectors for Salesforce, Workday, ServiceNow, SAP and other systems of record, and a large enterprise API.

Enterprise editions add workflow design, which lets an administrator build multi step sending processes with conditional routing, and web forms, which publish a signable document at a URL for anyone to complete. Bulk send, custom branding, advanced authentication for signers and a shared document library round out the business tiers.

## Where it shines

For organisations already standardised on Adobe and Microsoft, Acrobat Sign is the path of least resistance. Users do not learn a new tool, IT does not manage another vendor, and the Microsoft integrations are the most complete of any e-signature product. Sending a contract from a Teams chat or triggering a signature step from a Power Automate flow is genuinely convenient.

Compliance depth is the other reason to choose it. Regulated industries such as pharmaceuticals, banking and government need signature evidence that meets specific standards, and Adobe has the certifications and the audit documentation to satisfy those reviews. Government ID verification, knowledge based authentication and phone authentication are all available as signer identity checks.

The PDF handling is the best in the category, which is unsurprising given who makes it. Forms with existing PDF fields are recognised automatically, documents render accurately on every device, and the finished file is a properly structured PDF rather than a flattened image. Acrobat's generative AI features for summarising and asking questions about a document help you get oriented in a long agreement, though they are a reading aid, not a legal review, and a lawyer still needs to read the terms.

## Where it falls short

Pricing is confusing. Acrobat Sign is sold inside several different Acrobat bundles, as a standalone business product and as enterprise licences, and working out which one gives you a specific feature takes effort. The individual plans include only limited signing, and the features that make the product interesting, such as workflow design and the Microsoft integrations, need the business or enterprise editions.

The interface feels dated next to newer tools and the sending experience has more clicks than it needs. Teams that want a friendlier day to day experience for a modest volume of documents are better served by [Dropbox Sign](/tools/dropbox-sign/), and [DocuSign](/tools/docusign/) has a wider third party integration catalogue and a more active roadmap around agreement management and AI. Acrobat Sign also lacks proposal style content building, so sales teams that want pricing tables and a content library should look at [PandaDoc](/tools/pandadoc/) instead.

## Conclusion

Adobe Acrobat Sign is the sensible choice for enterprises that live in Microsoft 365 and Acrobat and that face compliance reviews on their signature process. The integrations and certifications are hard to match. Outside that profile it is a competent but unremarkable signature tool with a tangled price list, and smaller teams will be happier with something simpler.
