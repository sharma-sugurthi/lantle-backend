## What is Digits?

Digits is an AI-native general ledger built from the ground up rather than bolted onto legacy accounting software. Every transaction that flows in from banks, cards and payment processors is automatically categorized and reconciled in near real time. Financial statements - income statement, balance sheet, cash flow - update continuously as data lands, so there is no waiting until month-end to know where the business stands. A built-in AI assistant can explain line-item variances in plain language, answering questions like "why did software spend jump 40 percent this month?" without requiring users to dig through sub-ledgers.

The platform also produces shareable, interactive reports designed for investors, board members and advisors. Recipients can click into any number to see the underlying transactions - a significant upgrade over static PDF board decks. Digits was designed with accounting firms as distribution partners, giving practitioners a collaborative workspace where they can manage multiple startup clients from a single dashboard.

## Where it shines

Speed of close is the headline advantage. Because categorization and reconciliation happen continuously, the month-end close becomes a review step rather than a multi-day project. Founders and CFOs see current numbers instead of figures that are already three or four weeks old, which makes the data genuinely useful for decisions rather than purely backward-looking compliance.

The interactive reporting layer is another standout. Board packages that would normally take hours to assemble are generated automatically, and stakeholders can self-serve answers instead of emailing the finance team with follow-up questions. For accounting firms, the multi-client view and collaborative features make it practical to manage a portfolio of startup engagements without toggling between separate logins.

Digits also earns marks for transparency. The AI shows its work - users can see why a transaction was categorized a certain way and override it - which builds trust in the automation rather than forcing blind acceptance. For teams evaluating similar platforms, [Puzzle](/tools/puzzle/) offers a free-tier alternative aimed at early-stage startups, while [QuickBooks Intuit Assist](/tools/quickbooks-intuit-assist/) remains the default for companies that need the broadest ecosystem of third-party integrations.

## Where it falls short

The most obvious limitation is ecosystem maturity. QuickBooks and Xero have thousands of third-party apps, integrations and trained professionals. Digits is growing quickly, but its integration catalogue and community of experienced users are still smaller. Businesses that rely on niche industry add-ons may find gaps.

Switching general ledgers is never trivial. Migrating historical data, retraining team workflows and re-establishing integrations require real effort, regardless of how good the destination platform is. Companies mid-audit or mid-fundraise should time a migration carefully.

Pricing targets funded startups and their accounting partners, so bootstrapped micro-businesses will find cheaper options elsewhere. There is no free tier, which puts Digits at a disadvantage against competitors like Puzzle that let very early companies start at no cost.

Finally, complex multi-entity or international consolidation scenarios are still better served by enterprise-grade tools. Digits focuses on the startup and SMB segment and does not try to compete with NetSuite or Sage Intacct.

## Conclusion

Digits delivers on the promise of a modern, AI-first ledger where the books are always current and reports explain themselves. It is best suited to venture-backed startups and the accounting firms that serve them - teams that value speed, transparency and investor-ready reporting over the sheer breadth of a legacy ecosystem. Companies that need the widest possible integration catalogue should weigh that trade-off, but for its target audience Digits represents a genuine step forward from traditional bookkeeping software.
