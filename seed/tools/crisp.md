## What is Crisp?

Crisp is a customer messaging platform built by a small French company and aimed squarely at startups and small businesses. The centre of the product is a shared inbox that pulls in website chat, email, WhatsApp, Messenger, Instagram, Telegram and SMS, so a two or three person team can answer everything from one screen. Around that inbox sit a chatbot builder, a public help centre, a lightweight CRM with contact profiles and segments, and campaign tooling for outbound chat and email sequences.

The AI layer is called MagicReply. It drafts suggested answers for agents from past conversations and help centre content, and it can be switched into an autonomous mode that answers customers directly and escalates to a human when it is unsure. Pricing is per workspace rather than per seat on most plans, and a permanent free plan covers a couple of agents with the core inbox and chat widget.

## Where it shines

Value for money is the reason most teams pick Crisp. A plan that would cost hundreds a month per seat elsewhere is priced for the whole workspace here, and the free tier is genuinely usable for a solo founder answering website chat. Everything is self-serve, there is no sales call, and you can be live on your site within an hour.

Channel coverage is broad for the price. WhatsApp and Instagram are first-class channels rather than paid add-ons, and the widget itself is quick, customisable and pleasant on mobile. The native apps for iOS, Android, macOS and Windows are solid, which matters when the founder is also the support team and answering from a phone.

The chatbot builder is a visual flow editor that handles the usual jobs: qualifying leads, routing by page or language, collecting an email when the team is offline, and pointing people at help articles. Combined with the knowledge base, which is clean and quick to publish, it covers most pre-sales and first-line support for a small product.

The developer story is good. There is a full REST API, webhooks, a JavaScript SDK for pushing user data and events into the widget, and native integrations with Slack, HubSpot, Salesforce, Pipedrive, Zendesk, Shopify and WordPress.

## Where it falls short

Crisp is a messaging tool, not a help desk. Conversations can be assigned, tagged and marked resolved, but there is no real ticket lifecycle, no SLA management and reporting is thin. Once a team has a dozen agents and a queue that spans days, you will feel the gaps and will want [Freshchat](/tools/freshchat/) paired with a proper desk, or a full suite such as [Intercom](/tools/intercom/).

The AI agent is a step behind the leaders. MagicReply answers well from documents but it does not run multi-step tasks against your backend, and there is no resolution-level reporting to prove what it is worth. If autonomous resolution is the main goal, [Ada](/tools/ada/) or Fin from Intercom are in a different league, at a very different price.

Support for Crisp itself is chat based and can be slow at busy times, and the pace of feature releases means documentation sometimes lags the product. Larger companies will also miss SSO and granular permissions on the lower plans.

## Conclusion

Crisp is the sensible default for a startup or small business that needs chat, email and social support in one inbox without paying per seat. The free plan is real, the paid plans are cheap, the channel coverage is wide and the AI assist is useful for common questions. Choose it for the first few years of a support function, and expect to move on when ticketing, SLAs and deeper AI automation become requirements.
