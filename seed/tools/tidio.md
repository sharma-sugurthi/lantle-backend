## What is Tidio?

Tidio is a website chat widget with a bot attached, aimed at online stores and small businesses rather than support departments. You install it on Shopify, WooCommerce, WordPress or through a script tag, and visitors get a chat bubble that can be answered by a person from the Tidio inbox or by Lyro, the AI agent. Lyro reads your help pages and FAQ content and answers questions in natural language, handing over to a human when it is unsure or when the visitor asks for one.

Alongside Lyro there is a visual flow builder for rule-based bots, triggered by page visits, exit intent, cart value or time on site, which handles things like discount prompts, abandoned cart nudges and lead capture. The inbox unifies website chat, email, Messenger, Instagram and WhatsApp conversations. A free plan covers a limited number of conversations and Lyro replies each month, and paid plans raise those limits, with Lyro conversations sold as a separate metered add-on.

## Where it shines

Time to a working bot is measured in minutes. Point Lyro at your store URL, let it crawl the FAQ and shipping policy, and it starts answering order, delivery and returns questions the same day. For a shop owner with no support team, that is the entire value proposition, and it delivers it without a consultant or an integration project.

The ecommerce hooks are practical. On Shopify, Lyro and human agents can see order details and the visitor's cart from inside the conversation, and flows can recommend products or offer a code when someone hesitates at checkout. The overlap between support and sales is deliberate and it suits small stores where the same person does both.

Lyro's answering behaviour is conservative in a good way. It sticks to the sources you give it and declines to answer when nothing matches, which reduces the risk of a bot inventing a refund policy. The human handover is clean, with the transcript visible in the inbox and mobile apps for answering on the go.

The free plan is enough to evaluate the product properly and, for a very small site, to run on for some time. Pricing is transparent and self-serve, with no sales call required.

## Where it falls short

Tidio is not a help desk. There are no SLAs, no real ticket lifecycle, limited assignment rules and thin reporting. Once a business has several agents, a backlog and email as the main channel, the inbox starts to strain. At that point [Freshdesk](/tools/freshdesk/) provides proper ticketing at a similar price point, and larger operations will need [Zendesk](/tools/zendesk/).

Lyro is capable within a narrow scope. It answers from documents well, but it does not perform actions across backend systems the way Fin from [Intercom](/tools/intercom/) does with its task workflows, and its knowledge management tooling is basic. Expect it to handle common pre-sales and order status questions, not complex account changes.

Lyro conversation limits are easy to exhaust on a busy site, and the add-on cost can surprise owners who signed up for the free plan. Rule-based flows also need pruning over time; sites that stack many triggers end up with overlapping pop-ups that annoy visitors.

## Conclusion

Tidio is the sensible choice for an ecommerce store or small business that wants a website bot and live chat without building a support function. Lyro is quick to deploy, honest about its limits and well connected to Shopify. Treat it as a front door for customers rather than a system for managing support work, and move to a proper help desk when volume and team size demand one.
