## What is Zip?

Zip sits in front of the rest of your procurement and finance stack. An employee who wants to buy something, renew a contract or bring on a new vendor fills in a single intake form, and Zip works out who needs to approve it. That might mean a budget owner, legal for contract review, security for a vendor questionnaire, IT for a software access check and finance for the final sign-off. Each of those steps runs in parallel where it can and in sequence where it must, and the requester sees a visual map of where the request sits.

Once a purchase is approved, Zip pushes the result downstream. It can create a purchase order, kick off vendor onboarding, collect tax and banking details, and hand the finished record to an ERP such as NetSuite, Workday, Coupa or SAP. Zip also sells its own accounts payable and virtual card modules, so a company can run intake through payment in one system. AI features draft intake answers from uploaded quotes and contracts, extract terms from vendor agreements and flag duplicate or overlapping software requests.

## Where it shines

The intake experience is the reason Zip wins deals. Most procurement tools are built for procurement staff, and the requester has to learn a foreign interface to submit anything. Zip inverts that. The requester answers a short set of plain questions, often through Slack or Teams, and never has to know which teams get involved behind the scenes. Adoption follows from that. When the easy path is also the compliant path, shadow purchasing drops off.

Workflow configuration is done with a visual builder that non-technical admins can maintain. Conditions can branch on spend amount, category, department, data sensitivity or whether the vendor is new. Companies that previously tracked approvals across email threads and spreadsheet trackers find that Zip gives them an audit trail for the first time, which matters a great deal during SOC 2 or financial audits.

The ERP integrations are deep rather than cosmetic. Zip reads cost centres, GL accounts and vendor master records from the ledger and writes back purchase orders and vendor records, so the finance team is not re-keying approved requests. Renewal management is also strong: Zip captures contract end dates and notice periods at intake and surfaces upcoming renewals early enough to renegotiate rather than auto-renew by accident.

## Where it falls short

Zip is an enterprise product with enterprise pricing and an enterprise sales process. Companies under a few hundred employees will find the implementation effort and annual commitment hard to justify, and there is no self-serve tier to try it out. Implementation typically takes weeks of workshops to map out approval logic, and the value only appears once that logic is in place.

The AP and card modules are newer than the intake core and less mature than dedicated spend platforms. Companies that already run bill pay elsewhere often keep it there and use Zip strictly for intake and approvals, which is a perfectly good setup but means paying for two systems.

Zip also does nothing for the physical movement of goods. If procurement in your business means containers and customs declarations rather than software contracts and services, [Flexport](/tools/flexport/) is the relevant tool, and Zip would only handle the approval to spend the money. Scheduling the vendor calls that come out of a procurement process still needs something like [Calendly](/tools/calendly/).

## Conclusion

Zip is the best intake and orchestration layer available for companies whose purchasing has outgrown email approvals. It makes the compliant route the easy route, gives finance and legal a proper audit trail and connects cleanly to the ERPs that matter. Smaller companies should wait until the volume of requests justifies the cost, and anyone shopping primarily for payments should evaluate the AP module carefully before assuming it can replace an existing tool.
