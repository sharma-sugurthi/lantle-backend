## What is Semrush?

Semrush is a search marketing suite with one of the largest keyword and backlink databases in the industry. The core is competitive research: enter any domain and you can see the keywords it ranks for, the estimated traffic those rankings bring, the pages earning that traffic, the sites linking to it and the paid search ads it has run. Keyword Magic Tool expands a seed term into thousands of related queries with volume, difficulty and intent. Position Tracking monitors your rankings daily by location and device. Site Audit crawls your pages and reports technical issues by severity.

Beyond organic search, Semrush covers paid advertising research, a content marketing toolkit with an SEO writing assistant, local listing management, social media scheduling and a growing set of AI visibility tools that track how brands appear in AI generated answers. There is a free tier with tight daily limits, then tiered monthly subscriptions that add more projects, tracked keywords and reports, plus separately priced toolkits for local, content, social and AI features.

## Where it shines

Competitor research is the reason most people sign up and it remains the strongest part. Being able to reverse engineer a rival's organic strategy, see which pages carry their traffic, and pull their paid ad copy and landing pages saves weeks of guesswork when planning a quarter. The Keyword Gap and Backlink Gap reports turn that into a to do list of terms and link sources they have and you do not.

Breadth is genuinely useful for a small in-house team. One subscription covers keyword research, rank tracking, technical auditing, backlink monitoring and PPC research, so a two person team does not need four logins and four invoices. The Projects structure keeps all of that organised per site, with scheduled reports that can go straight to stakeholders.

Site Audit is thorough and explains issues in plain language with links to the affected pages. It is not a replacement for a dedicated crawler on very large sites, but for most business sites it catches the broken links, redirect chains, missing metadata and slow pages that matter.

The data updates frequently and the reporting is flexible. Custom PDF reports, a Looker Studio connector and an API mean agencies can white label deliverables without rebuilding them each month.

## Where it falls short

Cost climbs fast. The entry plan is enough for one site with modest tracking, but adding users, more tracked keywords, historical data or any of the toolkits pushes the bill up quickly, and the free tier is closer to a demo than a working plan. Teams often end up paying for breadth they do not use.

The interface is dense. There are dozens of reports with overlapping names and it takes weeks to learn which one answers a given question. New users frequently export the wrong metric or misread estimated traffic as actual traffic.

Volume and traffic figures are estimates modelled from clickstream and SERP data. They are directionally useful for comparing keywords and competitors, but treating them as precise numbers leads to bad forecasts. Check them against Search Console before making promises.

The content writing tools are the weakest part. The SEO Writing Assistant gives basic guidance, but teams serious about on-page optimisation will still want [Surfer](/tools/surfer/) for the editor, and copy production is better handled in [Jasper](/tools/jasper/) or a general model.

## Conclusion

Semrush is the sensible default for an in-house team that needs research, tracking and auditing in one place and has budget to match. Its competitor intelligence is hard to beat and the breadth means fewer tools to manage. Solo operators and content-only teams should look at whether a narrower tool covers their real needs before committing to a plan whose price rises with every added feature.
