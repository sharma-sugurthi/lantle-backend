## What is PandaDoc?

PandaDoc started as a proposal builder and grew into a full document workflow product with e-signature at the end of it. You assemble a document from a template in a drag and drop editor, pull reusable blocks from a content library, add pricing tables that calculate totals and optional line items, and send it to recipients who review, comment, sign and, if you turn it on, pay from the same link. Signatures are legally binding under ESIGN and UETA, and every completed document comes with an audit trail recording who opened, viewed and signed it.

The CRM connection is the reason most teams pick it. Native integrations with HubSpot, Salesforce, Pipedrive, Zoho and monday.com let a rep generate a quote or contract from a deal record with the contact, company and product fields already filled. Approval rules can hold a document until a manager signs off on a discount, and the analytics show how long each recipient spent on each page, which is useful feedback for anyone tuning a proposal.

Beyond sales, PandaDoc handles HR paperwork, NDAs, service agreements and forms. There is a permanent free plan for straightforward signing of uploaded documents, a paid tier that adds templates and the content library, and a business tier that adds CRM integrations, approval workflows and the API.

## Where it shines

Proposal and quote workflows are where PandaDoc is clearly stronger than plain signature tools. Pricing tables handle discounts, taxes, recurring versus one time charges and optional add ons that the customer can toggle before signing. The content library keeps case studies, bios and standard terms in one place so a rep is not copying from last month's proposal.

The recipient experience is good. Documents render as web pages rather than static PDFs, load quickly on a phone, and support comments in context, so a customer can question a line item without an email chain. Collecting a card payment at signature closes the loop for services businesses and removes a separate invoice step.

Template management is practical for teams. Roles, locked content blocks and workspace permissions stop reps editing legal language while still letting them personalise the parts that should change. The reporting on document status, time to sign and win rates by template is better than most signature first competitors offer.

## Where it falls short

If all you need is a signature on a PDF, PandaDoc is more product than you need, and the per seat cost of the tiers that include CRM integration adds up quickly. [DocuSign](/tools/docusign/) and [Dropbox Sign](/tools/dropbox-sign/) are simpler and cheaper for pure signing, and DocuSign is the more recognised name when counterparties are large companies with their own procurement rules.

The editor is web based and does not round trip cleanly with Microsoft Word. Legal teams that negotiate in Word with tracked changes will find themselves exporting, redlining and re-uploading, which loses the benefits of the template system. Redlining inside PandaDoc exists but is basic, and there is no clause level review help; the product formats and routes agreements, it does not assess them, and a lawyer still needs to read what is being signed.

Enterprise features such as SSO, higher API limits and bulk send sit on the top tier or as add ons, and support responsiveness on the lower plans is a common complaint.

## Conclusion

PandaDoc is the right choice when the document being signed is also being built, priced and negotiated in the same flow, which describes most sales teams. The CRM integrations, pricing tables and content library make it a proposal system that happens to include e-signature, and that is a stronger position than being a signature tool with a document editor bolted on. Teams that simply need signatures should look elsewhere and spend less.
