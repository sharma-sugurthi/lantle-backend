## What is Intercom?

Intercom started as an in-app messenger and has rebuilt itself around Fin, its AI agent. Fin sits in the messenger, in email and in the help centre, reads your knowledge base and past conversations, and answers customer questions directly. When it cannot resolve something it hands the conversation to a human in the inbox with a summary of what has already been tried. Intercom charges for Fin per resolved conversation, on top of per-seat pricing for human agents, so the bill scales with how much work the AI actually completes rather than how many questions it touches.

Around Fin sits a full support suite: a shared inbox with assignment rules and SLAs, a help centre, proactive messaging such as product tours and outbound emails, and a reporting layer. Copilot, the agent-facing assistant, drafts replies and searches internal content for human agents working in the inbox. Fin can also be deployed on top of Zendesk or Salesforce for teams that do not want to migrate their ticketing.

## Where it shines

Fin resolution quality is the reason to look at Intercom. Fed a well maintained help centre and a few months of conversation history, it answers a meaningful share of inbound chat without an agent touching it, and it is honest about what it does not know rather than inventing answers. The resolution-based pricing aligns incentives: if Fin performs badly you pay less, and the product team is clearly motivated to keep improving it.

The handover from AI to human is done well. The agent receives a transcript, a summary and the customer attributes Intercom already holds from the messenger, so the customer does not repeat themselves. Fin Tasks let you give the agent procedures, for example checking an order status through an API call before responding, which moves it from answering questions to actually completing work.

For SaaS products the messenger integration remains excellent. Customer data, events and segments flow in from the product, so support and onboarding messages can be targeted on real behaviour. The inbox is fast, keyboard driven and pleasant to work in for long shifts, which matters more than most buyers assume.

Reporting on Fin is specific: resolution rate, deflection, involvement rate and CSAT for AI versus human conversations, so you can see exactly what the automation is worth each month.

## Where it falls short

Cost is the recurring complaint. Seat prices are at the top of the category, Fin resolutions are billed on top, and proactive messaging features are priced separately again. A team with high volume and a low resolution rate can end up paying a lot for both the humans and the AI. Budgeting is harder than with flat per-seat tools because the Fin charge depends on customer behaviour.

Intercom is chat first. Voice is thin compared with dedicated contact centre tools, and ticketing for complex, multi-department cases with strict escalation paths feels lighter than [Zendesk](/tools/zendesk/), which has spent years on exactly that. If most of your volume is email and phone from a broad consumer base rather than chat from logged-in users, the messenger advantage largely disappears.

Small teams without a support function will find the platform heavy. A store or agency that mainly wants a bot on the website is better served by [Tidio](/tools/tidio/), and a budget conscious team building a help desk from scratch should compare [Freshdesk](/tools/freshdesk/) before committing.

## Conclusion

Intercom is the best choice when the goal is to have an AI agent resolve a large share of chat volume and keep humans for the remainder. Fin is the most capable support agent in this group, the handover is thoughtful and the data model suits SaaS companies. Accept that it is expensive and chat centric, and it earns the editors' pick.
