## What is Slite?

Slite is a team knowledge base built around a simple idea: a wiki is only useful if the content is trustworthy and easy to find. Documents are organised in channels and a page tree, the editor is block based and deliberately minimal, and the product spends its effort on the parts of a wiki most tools ignore. Every document can be verified by an owner with an expiry date, so readers see whether a policy is current, and a knowledge management panel lists stale pages, unverified pages and likely duplicates so someone can clean them up.

Ask is the AI layer. Team members type a question and receive an answer with citations to the source documents, and it can be reached from Slack so people do not have to open the wiki at all. AI also drafts and rewrites text inside the editor and can summarise long documents and discussion threads. Slite offers a trial rather than a permanent free plan, with pricing per member on a Standard tier and a Premium tier that adds advanced permissions, analytics and single sign on.

## Where it shines

Doc health is the feature that sells it. In most wikis, nobody knows whether the onboarding guide from two years ago is still accurate. In Slite, the owner is nudged to reverify, readers see the status, and admins get a list of what needs attention. That closes the loop that normally makes company wikis decay.

The AI answers are practical because the corpus is small and curated. A question about the expense policy or the deployment checklist gets a direct answer pointing at the verified page, and the Slack integration means new hires ask there instead of interrupting a colleague.

The editor is pleasant for writing. It is fast, clean, supports the usual embeds and tables, and the templates cover meeting notes, decisions and handbooks. Because there are no databases or project views, teams do not get distracted building trackers, which is exactly what some managers want from a wiki.

## Where it falls short

Slite is a wiki and nothing more. There are no relational databases, no task management beyond simple checklists, and no automations, so teams that want to run projects in the same tool will need something like [Notion](/tools/notion/) or [ClickUp](/tools/clickup/) alongside it. Integrations are limited to a modest set of embeds and the Slack connection, and the API is basic.

Pricing per member with no free plan makes it harder to justify for very small teams, or for companies where most staff only read the wiki occasionally. Larger organisations may find the permission model too simple compared with [Confluence](/tools/confluence/), and there is no self hosted option for regulated environments.

The company is small, which means a slower release cadence than the larger vendors and some risk that the product is acquired or repositioned. Mobile apps exist but are best for reading rather than writing.

## Conclusion

Slite is a good choice for a company of a few dozen to a few hundred people that wants a knowledge base to stay accurate and does not want the open ended flexibility of an all in one workspace. The verification and Ask features solve real problems that bigger tools leave to discipline. If you need databases, projects or a free plan, it is not the right fit.
