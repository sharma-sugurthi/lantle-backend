## What is Midjourney?

Midjourney is a text-to-image generator that started life as a Discord bot and now runs primarily through its own web app. You type a prompt and get a grid of four candidates back in under a minute. From there you can upscale, produce variations, extend the canvas in any direction, or open the built-in editor to repaint a selected region with a new prompt. Style references let you paste in an image and have new generations borrow its palette and rendering approach, and the personalisation feature learns your taste from images you rate over time.

There is no free tier. Every plan is a monthly or annual subscription that includes a pool of fast GPU time, with the higher tiers adding unlimited slower generations in relax mode and a stealth option that keeps your images out of the public feed. Commercial use is included for paying members, with the caveat that companies above a certain revenue threshold are required to be on the top plans. Midjourney also added a video mode that animates a still into a short clip, though image generation remains the reason most teams sign up.

## Where it shines

Aesthetic quality is still the thing that separates Midjourney from the field. Images come out with a coherence of light, texture and composition that most competitors struggle to match without heavy prompting. For a marketing team that needs a mood board, a campaign concept or a hero visual to react to before a shoot, it is the fastest route to something that looks considered rather than generated.

The web editor has closed most of the workflow gaps that made the Discord era painful. Inpainting a region, swapping a background, extending a landscape image to a portrait crop, and upscaling to print resolution all happen in one place. You can also upload your own photographs and retouch them with the same tools, which makes it useful for everyday retouching.

Style and character references matter for brand work. Once you have a look you like, you can pin it and produce dozens of variations that hold together as a set, which is what a campaign or a content calendar actually needs.

Community and discovery are underrated. The explore feed shows the prompts behind millions of images, and copying a structure that works is the fastest way to get competent.

## Where it falls short

Precise control is not Midjourney's strength. Rendering exact text, matching a specific product from several angles, or following a prompt with many spatial constraints remains unreliable. If the brief is an ad that must show your actual packaging, expect to composite in a separate tool. Layout and typography work belongs in [Figma](/tools/figma/), where generated imagery becomes one asset among many rather than the finished artwork.

There is no public API and no real team administration. Seats are individual subscriptions, shared asset libraries are thin, and anyone building generation into an internal pipeline will need to look elsewhere. Images are public by default on the lower tiers, which is a problem for confidential client work unless you pay for stealth mode.

The legal position on training data remains contested, and some enterprises have policies that rule out Midjourney output for anything customer facing. For teams whose real need is motion rather than stills, [Runway](/tools/runway/) is the more appropriate starting point.

## Conclusion

Midjourney remains the best image generator for teams that care about how things look and can live without pixel-level control. The web app, editor and reference tools have turned it from a curiosity into a daily driver for concept and campaign imagery. The lack of an API, weak team features and the legal grey area are real constraints, but for creative exploration and fast, striking visuals it is the one to beat.
