## What is HubSpot Service Hub?

HubSpot Service Hub is the support module of the HubSpot customer platform. It shares the same contact and company records as Marketing Hub and Sales Hub, so a support ticket sits on the same timeline as the emails a salesperson sent and the campaigns a contact opened. The help desk workspace is a ticket queue with a shared inbox behind it, taking email, live chat, Facebook Messenger, WhatsApp, web forms and calls placed through HubSpot's built in calling. A free tier includes basic ticketing, a shared inbox and live chat, and the paid tiers add SLAs, a knowledge base, a customer portal, surveys, routing and the more capable automation.

The AI is branded Breeze. Breeze customer agent answers chat and email conversations on its own using knowledge base articles and website content, and Breeze copilot sits with the agent to summarise, draft and recommend. Chatflows is the visual chatbot builder for menus and qualification, and it predates the AI agent, so both exist side by side. Pricing is per seat, with a lower cost view only seat for people who need to see tickets but not work them, and the AI agent consumes credits on top of the subscription.

## Where it shines

The CRM context is the reason to buy. An agent sees the deal, the renewal date, the account owner and every prior conversation without leaving the ticket, and a support issue can trigger a sales task or a marketing suppression with one workflow.

Workflows are the same automation engine the rest of HubSpot uses, and it is far more general than the trigger rules in a typical help desk. Tickets can be routed by property, escalated on SLA breach, enrolled in follow-up sequences and reported on alongside revenue. The reporting builder is strong: custom reports across tickets, contacts and deals are easy to make, and dashboards can be shared across departments.

The free tier is a real starting point for a small company that wants to stop doing support from a personal inbox, and the entry paid tier is cheap. Knowledge base, customer portal and surveys arrive on the middle tier and are well made, with multilingual articles and a feedback loop on which articles deflect tickets.

Breeze customer agent benefits from being trained on the same content your website and knowledge base already carry, and handover to a person keeps the full transcript in the ticket.

## Where it falls short

The valuable features sit two tiers up. SLAs, the knowledge base, the customer portal, surveys and Breeze customer agent all require the Professional tier, so the low entry price rarely reflects what a serious support team pays. Seat pricing plus AI credits plus onboarding fees makes the total cost harder to predict than a flat help desk.

Support depth is behind the specialists. Ticket views and macros are less refined than Zendesk, multi brand help centres are limited, voice relies on HubSpot calling minutes or a partner, and there is no equivalent of Zendesk's marketplace scale for support specific apps. The help desk workspace has improved a lot but still feels like a CRM that learned to do tickets.

If support is your primary product experience and you are not on HubSpot for sales or marketing, [Zendesk](/tools/zendesk/) or [Freshdesk](/tools/freshdesk/) will give you a better desk for the money, and ecommerce brands should look at [Gorgias](/tools/gorgias/). Teams that want a lighter shared inbox will prefer [Help Scout](/tools/help-scout/).

## Conclusion

HubSpot Service Hub is the obvious choice for a company that already runs sales and marketing on HubSpot and wants support on the same record. The shared CRM, the workflow engine and the reporting outweigh the gaps in pure help desk polish. Buy it for the platform, not as a standalone help desk, and budget for the Professional tier from the start.
