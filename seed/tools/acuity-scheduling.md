## What is Acuity Scheduling?

Acuity Scheduling is a booking system for businesses that sell time: therapists, coaches, massage clinics, photographers, trainers, tutors and consultants. Where a link-based tool assumes the meeting is free and the goal is to find a slot, Acuity assumes the appointment is a product. You define appointment types with a duration and a price, publish a scheduling page or embed it on your website, and clients book, fill in an intake form and pay in one flow. Squarespace owns Acuity, and it fits naturally into a Squarespace site, though it works with any website.

The commerce layer is what sets it apart. You can sell packages of sessions, gift certificates, subscriptions and memberships, and require deposits or full payment through Stripe, Square or PayPal. Classes and group sessions with capacity limits are supported alongside one-on-one appointments. Calendar sync covers Google, Outlook, iCloud and Office 365, and video links come from Zoom, Google Meet, Teams and others. Reminders go by email and text, and there is a client self-service portal for rescheduling.

## Where it shines

Intake forms are the strongest feature in the category. Every appointment type can carry its own questionnaire, with required fields, conditional questions and file uploads, so a new client's history, consent and preferences arrive before they walk in. Practitioners who used to chase paperwork on the day find this alone justifies the subscription. The top plan adds a business associate agreement for health providers who need one.

The payment and package tools are mature. Selling a bundle of ten sessions and having each booking draw down from it, or gating a class behind a membership, works without workarounds. No-show protection through required deposits is simple to set up.

Multi-staff and multi-location businesses are handled well for the price. Each calendar has its own hours, services and booking page, and an admin can book on behalf of clients, block time and see the whole business in one view. Recurring appointments, availability that varies by week and complex buffer rules are all there.

Client management is more than an afterthought. A client record shows history, notes, forms and payments, and the export options make it usable alongside a proper CRM.

## Where it falls short

Acuity is built for client bookings, not for internal or sales scheduling. There is no CRM ownership lookup, no routing forms and no weighted round robin, so a sales team routing inbound demos will find [Calendly](/tools/calendly/) far better suited. If the meeting is not something you charge for, most of Acuity's distinctive features go unused.

There is no free plan, only a trial. Individuals who just need a booking link will find cheaper or free options, including the free tiers of Calendly and [Cal.com](/tools/cal-com/).

The design has aged. The admin interface is dense and the client-facing scheduling page, while customisable in colour and layout, looks dated next to newer products. Configuration options are numerous and it is easy to end up with settings scattered across several menus. Text message reminders are limited or unavailable on the lower plans depending on region.

Integrations outside calendars, video and payments are thin. Mailchimp, Google Analytics and a few others exist natively, but most connections to other business software rely on Zapier.

## Conclusion

Acuity Scheduling is the sensible choice for a service business whose appointments are the product: intake forms, packages, deposits and memberships are all first class and the multi-staff support is generous for the money. It is the wrong tool for sales or recruiting teams, and individuals who only need a free link should look elsewhere. For the businesses it targets, it remains one of the most complete options available.
