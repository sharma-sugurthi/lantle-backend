## What is Dropbox Sign?

Dropbox Sign is the e-signature product that used to be HelloSign, bought by Dropbox in 2019 and renamed in 2022. The core workflow is deliberately plain. Upload a PDF or Word file, drop signature, date, text and checkbox fields where they belong, assign each to a signer, set a signing order if it matters, and send. Signers complete it in a browser or on a phone with no account. The finished file carries an audit trail listing every view, signature and IP address, and signatures are enforceable under ESIGN, UETA and eIDAS.

Templates cover the recurring documents, and once a template exists you send it by filling in signer names and a few merge fields. Bulk send handles the annual policy acknowledgement or the batch of contractor agreements. Integration with Dropbox itself is the obvious one: files can be sent for signature from a Dropbox folder and the executed copy lands back in it. There are also connectors for Google Drive, Google Docs, Gmail, Microsoft Word, Slack, Salesforce, HubSpot and Oracle.

The other half of the product is the API, which was HelloSign's original reason for existing. Developers embed signing, template editing and request creation inside their own applications, with white labelling on higher API tiers, and the documentation and SDKs are among the clearest in the category.

## Where it shines

Simplicity is the main strength. A new user can send a document within a couple of minutes without reading anything, and the field placement editor does not bury options in menus.

The API is the strongest reason developers choose Dropbox Sign over the alternatives. Embedded signing works from an iframe with a short lived URL, webhooks report events reliably, the test mode does not consume paid quota, and the SDKs cover the common languages. Product teams that need a signature step inside their own onboarding or checkout flow tend to get it working faster here than with DocuSign's larger and more configuration heavy API.

The free plan allows a few signature requests a month, which suits freelancers and very small businesses that occasionally need a signed agreement. Signer attachments, data validation on fields and qualified electronic signature support for EU customers are all present on the paid business plans, so growth does not immediately force a migration.

## Where it falls short

The product has not moved much since the acquisition. Feature velocity is slow, and there is nothing comparable to the agreement analysis, AI extraction or workflow builders that [DocuSign](/tools/docusign/) has added. Dropbox Sign is a signature tool and nothing more, which is fine until a team needs conditional fields, complex routing or a document repository with search.

Advanced sending features are thin. Conditional logic and calculated fields are absent or limited, approval steps before sending do not exist, and reporting stops at document status. Teams building proposals or quotes will do better with [PandaDoc](/tools/pandadoc/). Enterprise administration, including SSO and detailed user permissions, is available but not as deep as Adobe or DocuSign offer, and identity verification options for signers are narrower.

Pricing for the standard web plans is competitive, but the API tiers scale on request volume, so an application that sends thousands of documents a month can end up paying more than the same volume through the web app.

## Conclusion

Dropbox Sign is the easiest e-signature tool to get started with and one of the best APIs to build on. Choose it if you are a small team that wants signing without configuration, or a product team embedding signature into your own software. Larger organisations that need workflow logic, identity checks and a searchable agreement archive will find it too limited and should look at DocuSign or Adobe.
