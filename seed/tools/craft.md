## What is Craft?

Craft is a document and note taking app that started on Mac, iPhone and iPad and has since added Windows and a web version. Documents are made of blocks and can be nested into subpages, grouped into folders and spaces, and linked to each other with backlinks. The design is the first thing people notice: typography, page styles, cards and colour themes make documents look finished with little effort, and the native apps feel like they belong on the platform rather than being a wrapped website.

Craft stores documents locally and syncs them through its own service, so everything works offline and loads instantly. Any page can be shared as a public link with a clean reader view, which makes it a quick way to publish a proposal, a portfolio page or a set of release notes. Daily notes, tasks with due dates and a calendar view cover personal planning, and the AI assistant drafts, rewrites, translates and summarises within a document. There is a free plan with a limited number of blocks and documents, personal paid plans that remove the limits and add storage and AI usage, and team plans with shared spaces and administration.

## Where it shines

Writing feels good. The editor is responsive, the keyboard shortcuts are thorough, and the block model stays out of the way when you just want to type. For people who found Notion cluttered, Craft is the closest alternative that keeps the block flexibility while restoring focus.

Offline behaviour is reliable in a way most cloud editors are not. Work on a plane, and the document is exactly where you left it when you reconnect. On an iPad with a keyboard it is one of the best writing environments available.

Sharing is well thought out. A shared document looks like a small website, supports comments from readers, and can be password protected or set to expire. Small consultancies and freelancers use this instead of building a client portal.

Export to Markdown, PDF and Word is clean, and the Markdown output is close enough to the source that moving out of Craft later is realistic rather than theoretical.

## Where it falls short

Craft has no real databases. Collections offer a table view over documents with properties, but there are no relations, rollups or formulas, so anyone running a content calendar or a lightweight CRM in [Notion](/tools/notion/) will hit a wall quickly. Real time collaboration works but is less mature than in tools built for teams from the start, and the permission model is simple.

Automations and integrations are thin. There is a basic API, some calendar and task app connections, and embeds, but nothing like the Packs in [Coda](/tools/coda/) or the marketplace around [Confluence](/tools/confluence/). Search is fine within a space but does not reach into other tools.

The free plan limits are tight enough that regular users end up paying, and the team tier is priced per member, which can be hard to justify when the collaboration features trail competitors. The product also changes its plan structure fairly often, which is worth checking before committing a team.

## Conclusion

Craft is the tool for people who care how their documents look and want a fast, offline capable editor on Mac, iPad and now Windows. It is an excellent personal and small team writing app and a poor substitute for a database driven workspace or a large company wiki. Choose it for the writing experience and the sharing, not for structure or integrations.
