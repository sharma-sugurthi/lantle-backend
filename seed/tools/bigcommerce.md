## What is BigCommerce?

BigCommerce is a hosted, SaaS ecommerce platform in the same broad category as Shopify. You subscribe, pick a theme, load your catalogue and the company runs the storefront, checkout, hosting and security. It comes in two tiers: the self-serve plans aimed at small and growing stores, and an Enterprise tier with negotiated pricing, a dedicated account team and higher limits.

Where it differs is philosophy. BigCommerce tries to build the things merchants commonly need into the core product rather than leaving them to an app store. Faceted search, customer groups with tiered pricing, product options with many variants, unlimited staff accounts, a native B2B edition and multi-storefront management from one control panel all come with the platform. It also leans hard into headless commerce, with GraphQL and REST APIs designed so you can run the storefront on Next.js, WordPress or a CMS while BigCommerce handles the cart, catalogue and orders behind it.

## Where it shines

Catalogue depth is the strongest argument. Products can carry hundreds of variants, custom fields, modifiers that change price, bulk pricing tiers and per-customer-group pricing without a single app installed. For wholesalers, distributors and stores with complex configurable products, this removes the metafield workarounds that other platforms require.

There is no extra transaction fee for using your own payment gateway. BigCommerce supports a wide list of processors and does not penalise you for choosing one, which matters for merchants with an existing acquiring relationship or trading in regions where the platform's default option is a poor fit.

Multi-storefront lets one account run several branded storefronts, each with its own domain, theme, catalogue subset and pricing, while sharing inventory and order management. Combined with the Channel Manager, which pushes listings to Amazon, eBay, Walmart, Google, Facebook and Instagram, and the Feedonomics feed engine the company owns, it is a practical hub for brands selling in many places at once.

The headless story is credible. The APIs are complete, the Catalyst reference storefront gives developers a real starting point, and the platform does not fight you when you want the front end somewhere else.

## Where it falls short

The theme and app ecosystem is a fraction of Shopify's size. There are good themes and the essential apps exist, but for niche needs you will find fewer choices, and fewer agencies specialise in the platform. Stencil, the theme framework, is capable but has a smaller community and less current documentation than Liquid.

The self-serve plans have annual sales thresholds. Once a store's trailing twelve-month revenue passes a plan's ceiling, it is moved up to the next plan automatically, so a good year raises your subscription without you doing anything.

The admin feels older and busier than newer competitors, and native AI assistance in the back office lags behind what Shopify now ships. Storefront merchandising is also more manual: there is no built-in recommendation engine of note, so personalised upsells still mean an app. If you want the largest ecosystem and the smoothest checkout, [Shopify](/tools/shopify/) remains the safer default, and if you want to own everything outright, [WooCommerce](/tools/woocommerce/) is the open source route.

## Conclusion

BigCommerce is the hosted platform for merchants whose catalogue or customer structure is too complicated for a beginner-oriented builder. B2B sellers, multi-brand operators and teams planning a headless front end get a lot of capability in the base subscription and no gateway penalty. Small stores with simple catalogues will get more polish and more apps elsewhere, and that is the honest line between the two.
