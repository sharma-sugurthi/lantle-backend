## What is Rebuy?

Rebuy is a personalisation and merchandising app for Shopify stores. It sits on top of the storefront and decides which products to show a given shopper at each step: recommended items on the product page, add-ons in the cart, a post-purchase offer after checkout, and a reorder prompt in the account area. The recommendations come from a mix of machine learning trained on your order history and rules you write yourself, so you can let the model choose or pin exactly what appears.

The best known piece is the Smart Cart, a slide-out cart that replaces the theme's default. It carries progress bars toward free shipping thresholds, tiered gift-with-purchase offers, subscription upgrade toggles and a recommendation carousel, all configurable from the Rebuy admin without touching theme code. Around it sit widgets for dynamic bundles, product page upsells, a smart search, and a post-purchase page that appears between checkout and the thank-you screen. Rebuy is built for Shopify and Shopify Plus specifically; it is not a platform-agnostic tool.

## Where it shines

The rules engine is what separates Rebuy from a simple recommendations plugin. You can filter suggestions by tag, collection, price band, inventory level or metafield, exclude items already in the cart, and layer conditions on top of the AI ranking. A merchant selling skincare can, for instance, only recommend a serum when the cart contains a cleanser from the same line and the shopper has not bought it before. Getting that level of control usually means custom development elsewhere.

Smart Cart earns its place. Free shipping progress bars and tiered offers are simple ideas, but having them work reliably across themes, respect discount codes and update instantly is harder than it sounds, and Rebuy's implementation handles the edge cases well. Average order value gains from the cart alone often justify the subscription.

The A/B testing built into the app lets you test one widget configuration against another and see revenue impact per variant rather than guessing. Integrations with subscription apps such as Recharge, plus review and loyalty tools, mean recommendations respect subscription status and can surface loyalty offers in context.

Support and onboarding are hands-on for larger plans, and the team will help set up an initial merchandising strategy rather than leaving you with an empty dashboard.

## Where it falls short

Pricing scales with store volume, which is fair in principle but stings for high-volume stores with thin margins. A merchant with large order counts and low average order values can end up paying a lot for widgets that add a small percentage per order. Do the maths on your own numbers before committing.

Performance needs attention. Rebuy injects a fair amount of JavaScript, and on a theme that already carries several apps the storefront can slow down noticeably. Careful configuration and lazy loading help, but it is not a set-and-forget install for teams that care about Core Web Vitals.

The AI recommendations are only as good as your order history. New stores or stores with narrow catalogues get generic results, and you will rely heavily on manual rules for the first few months. The admin has a learning curve too, with many overlapping settings spread across widgets, data sources and rulesets.

If you need the hosting and checkout foundation first, that is [Shopify](/tools/shopify/) itself; Rebuy adds to it rather than replacing anything. Merchants whose real problem is pricing strategy rather than product placement should look at [Prisync](/tools/prisync/) instead.

## Conclusion

Rebuy is the most complete merchandising layer available for Shopify, and the Smart Cart plus rules engine combination pays back quickly for stores with decent traffic and a catalogue that lends itself to cross-selling. Small stores with limited data and tight margins should wait until there is enough volume to justify the cost and enough history to feed the model.
