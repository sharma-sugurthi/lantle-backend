## What is Apollo?

Apollo combines a large B2B contact and company database with the tools to act on it: email sequences, a dialer, LinkedIn task steps, meeting scheduling and a light CRM. You filter the database by title, industry, headcount, technology used, funding stage or recent job change, save the results as a list, and push those people straight into a multi-step sequence without exporting a spreadsheet. Email addresses and, at higher tiers, mobile numbers are revealed against a monthly credit allowance.

The platform has grown well past a data provider. Intent signals show which accounts are researching topics related to your category, website visitor identification names the companies browsing your site, and an AI assistant drafts personalised opening lines from the data on the record. There is a Chrome extension for revealing contacts from LinkedIn profiles and company websites, and two-way syncs with HubSpot and Salesforce keep the source of truth in your CRM if you already have one. The free plan gives a small monthly credit allowance and basic sequencing, which is enough to test whether the data quality holds up for your market.

## Where it shines

Value for money is the main reason Apollo is the default recommendation for early outbound teams. A traditional data subscription plus a separate sequencing tool costs several times what an Apollo seat does, and the combined workflow means fewer integrations to babysit. For a two or three person team building their first outbound motion, it is difficult to justify anything else.

The search filters are deep and fast. Being able to build a list of heads of finance at companies that use a specific accounting platform, raised money in the last year and have hired in the past month, then sequence that list within minutes, is the core loop and it works. Saved searches surface new matches as the database updates.

Sequencing is solid for a bundled tool. A/B testing on steps, reply detection, automatic bounce handling, sending windows and mailbox rotation are all present. The built in deliverability tools, including inbox warm-up and domain health checks, are useful for teams that have never managed sending reputation before.

Coverage of US contacts, particularly in technology and services companies, is strong, and email accuracy in that segment is competitive with far more expensive providers.

## Where it falls short

Data quality is uneven outside its strongest segments. European and Asia Pacific coverage is thinner, mobile numbers are hit and miss, and small companies or non-technology industries return more stale records. Many teams verify with a second source before dialing, which eats into the cost advantage.

The CRM and deal tracking features are functional but thin. Apollo is not a replacement for a proper CRM once you have a pipeline with multiple stakeholders and a renewal motion, and most teams end up syncing it to [HubSpot Sales Hub](/tools/hubspot-sales-hub/) or Salesforce anyway. The sync itself works, but field mapping and duplicate handling need care during setup.

Personalisation is limited to what Apollo knows about the contact. If your outreach depends on signals from many sources, such as job postings, podcast appearances or product reviews, a workflow builder like [Clay](/tools/clay/) offers far more flexibility, at the cost of building and maintaining the tables yourself. Apollo also suffers from its own popularity: so many teams send the same templates from it that generic sequences underperform, and recipients have learned to spot them.

## Conclusion

Apollo is the best starting point for outbound teams that need data and sequencing without a large budget. The database is broad, the search is excellent, and the free and lower paid tiers let you prove the motion before committing. Its limits are data coverage outside the US technology segment and a CRM that will not carry you far. Treat it as a prospecting engine feeding a real CRM and it earns its place.
